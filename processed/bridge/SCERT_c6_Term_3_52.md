## What you already know

Your clothes, a rope used to tie things, and a soft mattress to sleep on all come from plants or plant parts. Some of these plant materials can be turned into thread, rope or cloth.

## Lesson

Some plants give us fibres. A **fibre** is a long, thin thread-like part. Plants that give us the fibres we need for our uses are called **fibre yielding plants**.

The fibres from these plants can be **spun** (twisted together) into **thread**, **rope** and **cloth**. These fibres are called **natural fibres**, because they come from nature.

We can sort fibre yielding plants in two ways.

**1. Sorting by use**

- **Textile fibres** are used to make cloth. Cotton is an example.
- **Cordage fibres** are used to make rope. Coconut fibre is an example.
- **Filling fibres** are used to fill mattresses. Silk cotton is an example.

**2. Sorting by the part of the plant**

- **Plant fibres** come from seed hairs. Cotton is an example.
- **Stem fibres**, also called **bast fibres**, come from the stem. Flax and jute are examples.
- **Leaf fibres** come from leaves. Agave is an example.
- **Husk fibres** come from the husk, the outer covering. Coconut is an example.

**An example from daily life**

Jute is a stem fibre plant. In India, jute is grown in seven states. West Bengal alone produces over 50% of the raw jute in the country. Raw jute is the plant fibre before it is made into products.

Remember: a fibre yielding plant gives us a fibre. The fibre can be spun into thread, rope or cloth.

## Key words

- **Fibre**: a long, thin, thread-like part of a plant.
- **Fibre yielding plant**: a plant that gives us fibres for our uses.
- **Natural fibre**: a fibre that comes from nature, such as from a plant.
- **Spun**: twisted together to make thread.
- **Textile fibre**: a fibre used to make cloth.
- **Cordage fibre**: a fibre used to make rope.
- **Filling fibre**: a fibre used to fill mattresses.
- **Husk**: the outer covering of a plant part, such as a coconut.
- **Stem fibre (bast fibre)**: a fibre that comes from the stem of a plant.
- **Raw jute**: jute in its natural form before it is made into products.

## Practice questions

1. What are fibre yielding plants?
2. Name the fibre used to make cloth.
3. Which part of the coconut plant gives us fibre?
4. Which type of fibre is used to fill mattresses, and what is one example?
5. Name one thing at home that is made from cotton.

## Answers

1. Fibre yielding plants are plants that give us the fibres we need for our uses.
2. Cotton is a textile fibre, and textile fibres are used to make cloth.
3. The husk of the coconut gives us husk fibre.
4. Filling fibres are used to fill mattresses. Silk cotton is one example.
5. Cloth made from cotton, such as a shirt, is one example.
