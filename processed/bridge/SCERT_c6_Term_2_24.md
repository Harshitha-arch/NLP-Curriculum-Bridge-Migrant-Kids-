## What you already know
You have seen ice melt into water and water turn into vapour when heated (from your NCERT book). In this unit, you will learn to name many more changes you see every day and sort them into groups.

## Lesson
Look around you today. Things are changing all the time. Milk goes sour. A wet floor dries. A candle burns down. A leaf falls from a tree. These are all changes.

In this unit, we learn to name changes and sort them into groups. Each change can be placed in more than one group. Here are the pairs of groups you need to know.

**Slow or fast.** A slow change takes a long time. A fast change happens quickly.

**Reversible or irreversible.** A reversible change can be undone, so the material can go back to how it was. An irreversible change cannot be undone.

**Physical or chemical.** A physical change changes the look or state of a material, but it does not make a new material. A chemical change makes a new material.

**Desirable or undesirable.** A desirable change is one that we want. An undesirable change is one that we do not want.

**Natural or human-made.** A natural change happens by itself in nature. A human-made change is caused by people.

Let us try one. When ice melts into water, the ice changes its state. Can the water be frozen back into ice? Yes. So this change is reversible. Is it fast or slow? It depends on the place and the heat, so you must look at each case.

Now two more words. **Dissolution** is the process of a material dissolving. In a dissolving process, we have a **solvent** and a **solute**. Your teacher will show you the examples from your textbook to tell them apart.

Remember: for every change you see, ask these questions. Is it slow or fast? Can it be reversed? Is it physical or chemical? Do we want it? Did nature do it or people?

## Key words
- **reversible**: a change that can be undone
- **irreversible**: a change that cannot be undone
- **physical change**: a change that does not make a new material
- **chemical change**: a change that makes a new material
- **desirable**: something we want
- **undesirable**: something we do not want
- **natural**: happening by itself in nature
- **human-made**: caused by people
- **solvent**: the liquid that does the dissolving (as used in your textbook)
- **solute**: the material that dissolves in the solvent (as used in your textbook)

## Practice questions
1. Name one change you saw at home today.
2. Is a change that can be undone called reversible or irreversible?
3. What is the difference between a physical change and a chemical change?
4. Ice melts into water. Is this change reversible? Why?
5. A pot of food gets burnt on the stove. Is this change desirable or undesirable? Why?

## Answers
1. Any one change you saw at home, such as a wet floor drying or a candle burning down.
2. It is called reversible.
3. A physical change does not make a new material, but a chemical change makes a new material.
4. Yes, it is reversible, because water can be frozen back into ice.
5. It is undesirable, because we do not want our food to burn.
