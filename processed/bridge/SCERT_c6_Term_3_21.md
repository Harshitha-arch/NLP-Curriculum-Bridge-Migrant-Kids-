## What you already know

You learned in NCERT that rivers, lakes and ground water are sources of freshwater that we use every day. This lesson tells you what happens to that water before it reaches our homes.

## Lesson

Think about the water that comes out of the tap in your home. Where does it come from? You may say the tap, but the water first comes from a source such as a river, a lake or the ground.

You already know these sources. Water is needed for drinking, cooking, bathing and washing. Water is precious, so we must use it well.

In some areas, water from a river, a lake or ground water is **treated** before people use it. Treated means the water is made clean so that it is safe to use. The passage does not tell us how this is done, so you only need to know that it is treated.

After treatment, the water is **distributed**. Distributed means it is shared out and sent to many places, such as homes. In many areas, this work is done by **local bodies**. Local bodies are the offices that look after a town or village.

So the path of water in these areas is:

- Water is taken from a river, a lake or the ground.
- The water is treated at a **treatment plant**. A treatment plant is a place where water is treated.
- Local bodies distribute the treated water to people.

Your textbook shows a **model** of a water distribution and treatment plant. A model is a small copy of a real thing. It helps us understand how the real thing works. Look at the figures and follow how the water moves through the model.

Next time you turn on a tap, think about the journey the water has made to reach you.

## Key words

- **local bodies**: the offices that look after a town or village
- **treated**: made clean so that it is safe to use
- **distributed**: shared out and sent to many places
- **treatment plant**: a place where water is treated
- **model**: a small copy of a real thing that helps us understand it
- **ground water**: water that is found under the ground

## Practice questions

1. Name the three sources of water that are treated and distributed in some areas.
2. Who distributes the water in many areas?
3. What does "treated" mean?
4. Describe, in your own words, how water from a river can reach your home.
5. Why is a model of a treatment plant useful?

## Answers

1. The three sources are river, lake and ground water.
2. Local bodies distribute the water in many areas.
3. Treated means the water is made clean so that it is safe to use.
4. Water from a river is treated at a treatment plant, and then local bodies distribute it to homes.
5. A model is a small copy of a real thing, so it helps us understand how the real plant works.

## Note for the teacher

This lesson is based only on the short SCERT passage, with NCERT used just to link sources of water that the child has already studied. The SCERT passage does not explain how water is treated, does not name the local bodies, and refers to figures that are not included, so these parts were left out.
