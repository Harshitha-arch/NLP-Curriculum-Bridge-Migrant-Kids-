## What you already know
You may have seen a magnet that keeps a pencil box or purse closed. Sailors in the olden days used a magnetic compass to find directions.

## Lesson
Magnets are useful in many everyday things. Look at this list:

- Phone covers
- Pin holders
- Magnetic crane
- Some toys
- Compasses
- Pencil boxes
- Stickers on refrigerators
- Speakers
- Small electric motors
- Some door locks
- Bags

Pencil boxes and purses use magnets to keep them closed. A compass uses a magnet to help people find directions.

Some very fast trains also use magnets. This is called an **electromagnetic train**. It is also called a **suspension train** or a **flying train**. It does not need diesel or petrol to run. It uses two magnetic effects: **attraction** and **repulsion**.

**Attraction** means two magnets pull towards each other. **Repulsion** means two magnets push away from each other. The electromagnetic train works with these pulls and pushes.

Talk with your friends. Which of these things do you use or see every day? Can you find a magnet in your bag or pencil box?

## Key words
- **magnet**: a thing that can pull some objects towards it
- **magnetic attraction**: the pull between two magnets
- **repulsion**: the push between two magnets that pushes them apart
- **electromagnetic train**: a very fast train that works with magnets and does not need diesel or petrol
- **suspension train**: another name for the electromagnetic train
- **flying train**: another name for the electromagnetic train
- **compass**: a tool that helps people find directions
- **speaker**: a device that makes sound
- **electric motor**: a machine that uses electricity to make things move
- **toy**: something children play with

## Practice questions
1. Name two things from the list that use magnets.
2. What are the other two names for the electromagnetic train?
3. Which two magnetic effects does the electromagnetic train use to run?
4. What is the difference between attraction and repulsion?
5. Why is a magnet useful in a pencil box?

## Answers
1. Any two from the list, such as pencil boxes, compasses, phone covers, or speakers.
2. The electromagnetic train is also called the suspension train and the flying train.
3. It uses magnetic attraction and repulsion.
4. Attraction is a pull between two magnets. Repulsion is a push between two magnets.
5. A magnet helps keep the pencil box closed.
