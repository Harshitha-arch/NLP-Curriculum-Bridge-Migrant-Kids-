## What you already know
You learned in NCERT that petrol, diesel, coal and natural gas are fossil fuels. You also learned that when they burn, smoke and carbon dioxide gas are produced, and this pollutes the air.

## Lesson
Air pollution means that the air around us has become dirty and harmful. Most air pollution is caused by burning fossil fuels.

You already know some fossil fuels: petrol, diesel, coal and natural gas. Today we learn where these fuels are burnt in large amounts.

There are three main places:

1. **Industries**: factories where things are made.
2. **Power plants**: places that make electricity. Coal is mainly used to make electricity.
3. **Motor vehicles**: cars, buses, lorries and bikes.

Think about a bus on a busy road. It burns petrol or diesel. Smoke comes out of its exhaust pipe. A factory and a power plant also burn fuel, so they add to the dirty air.

When fossil fuels burn, they release **toxic gases**. Toxic means poisonous or harmful to living things. These gases spoil the air we breathe.

So, to remember this lesson: fossil fuels are burnt in industries, power plants and motor vehicles. Burning them releases toxic gases into the air, and this causes air pollution.

## Key words
- **Air pollution**: dirty or harmful things in the air.
- **Fossil fuels**: oil, petrol, coal and natural gas.
- **Industries**: factories where things are made.
- **Power plants**: places that make electricity.
- **Motor vehicles**: cars, buses, lorries and bikes.
- **Toxic gases**: gases that are poisonous or harmful.

## Practice questions
1. Name the four fossil fuels given in the lesson.
2. Name the three places where fossil fuels are burnt.
3. What kinds of gases are released when fossil fuels burn?
4. Why does burning fossil fuels cause air pollution?
5. Look around your street. Name one thing near your home that burns fossil fuels.

## Answers
1. The four fossil fuels are oil, petrol, coal and natural gas.
2. Fossil fuels are burnt in industries, power plants and motor vehicles.
3. Burning fossil fuels releases toxic gases.
4. The toxic gases released by burning fossil fuels make the air dirty and harmful, which causes air pollution.
5. Answers will vary. Examples: a bus, a lorry, a bike, or a factory near the home.

## Note for the teacher
This lesson is based on the air pollution section of the SCERT passage, with NCERT's fossil fuel facts used only in "What you already know"; the water pollution section was left out because the topic is air.
