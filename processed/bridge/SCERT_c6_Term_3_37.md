## What you already know

You have seen that fish live in water and goats live on land, and that each has a body suited to where it lives. In this lesson, you will learn that living things and non-living things in one place form a single system called an ecosystem.

## Lesson

Look around your home. You have books, toys, furniture, food, and maybe pets. Some of these things are alive, like pets. Others are not alive, like books and furniture. Together, all of these living and non-living things make up your home.

Scientists have a name for a place like this, where living and non-living things work together. It is called an **ecosystem**. An ecosystem is a community of living and non-living things that work together. Each part of an ecosystem has a role to play.

Living things in an ecosystem are called **biotic components**. These include plants, animals, and human beings.

Non-living things are called **abiotic components**. They include:

- **Soil.** Soil has water, air, and organic matter in it. These are called **edaphic factors**.
- **Physical factors.** These are light, temperature, wind, and humidity.

Ecosystems can be **natural** or **artificial**.

A **natural ecosystem** is made without human help. A natural ecosystem can be **aquatic**, which means it is in water. Sea, river, lake, pond, and puddle are examples. It can also be **terrestrial**, which means it is on land. Forests, mountain regions, and deserts are examples.

An **artificial ecosystem** is created and kept going by people. It is much simpler than a natural ecosystem. Paddy fields and gardens are terrestrial artificial ecosystems. A fish tank is an aquatic artificial ecosystem.

Changes in the environment can have a big impact on an ecosystem. For example, a rise in temperature or heavy rains can change how an ecosystem works.

## Key words

- **Ecosystem**: a community of living and non-living things that work together
- **Biotic**: living things in an ecosystem
- **Abiotic**: non-living things in an ecosystem
- **Aquatic**: found in water
- **Terrestrial**: found on land
- **Natural ecosystem**: an ecosystem made without human help
- **Artificial ecosystem**: an ecosystem created and kept going by people
- **Edaphic factors**: the soil-related non-living parts of an ecosystem (water, air, and organic matter in soil)
- **Physical factors**: light, temperature, wind, and humidity
- **Humidity**: the amount of moisture in the air

## Practice questions

1. What is an ecosystem?
2. Name two biotic components of an ecosystem.
3. Why is a fish tank called an artificial ecosystem, while a forest is called a natural ecosystem?
4. Your home is made of living and non-living things. Name one living thing and one non-living thing from your home.
5. What might happen to an ecosystem if its temperature rises a lot or there are heavy rains?

## Answers

1. An ecosystem is a community of living and non-living things that work together.
2. Any two of these: plants, animals, and human beings.
3. A fish tank is created and kept going by people, so it is artificial. A forest is not made by people, so it is natural.
4. Any pair, for example: a pet (living) and a book (non-living).
5. It can have a big impact on the ecosystem.
