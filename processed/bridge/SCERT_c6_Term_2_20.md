## What you already know

You use wires, plugs and switches every day, and you know that touching a bare live wire can give a shock. Some things, like metal, let electricity pass through them, and some things, like plastic, do not.

## Lesson

Electricity moves through some materials, but not through others. Today we learn which materials let it pass and which do not.

Look inside an electric wire. If the wire is cut, you can see a metal wire inside. Around the metal is another material. This outer covering stops the electricity from escaping. Why is this? We will learn more about it.

Some words first:

- A **conductor** is a material that lets electric current pass through it.
- An **insulator** is a material that does not let electric current pass through it. We also call it a **non-conducting material**.

In an activity, you connect objects between points A and B in a circuit. Then you watch the bulb. If the bulb glows, current is passing through the object. If the bulb does not glow, current is not passing through. The objects in the activity include a pin, a match stick, a safety pin, a pencil, a metal spoon, a rubber, a pen, a wooden scale, a hairpin and a glass piece. Test each one and write down whether the bulb glows or not.

Electricity can be dangerous. If someone gets an electric shock, follow these safety steps:

1. Switch off the power supply.
2. Remove the connection from the switch.
3. Push the person away using a non-conducting material. Never use a conductor, such as a metal rod, to do this.
4. Give first aid and take the person to the nearest health centre.

Remember: conductors let current pass, and insulators stop it.

## Key words

- **Conductor**: a material that lets electric current pass through it.
- **Insulator**: a material that does not let electric current pass through it.
- **Non-conducting material**: a material that electric current cannot pass through; another name for an insulator.
- **Electric current**: the flow of electricity through a wire or circuit.
- **Circuit**: a closed path that electric current can travel around.
- **Electric shock**: a sudden pain or jolt caused when electric current passes through a person's body.
- **Switch**: a part that turns the electricity on or off.
- **Power supply**: the source that provides electricity to a circuit or appliance.

## Practice questions

1. Inside an electric wire, what surrounds the metal wire?
2. In the activity, what does it mean when the bulb glows?
3. If someone gets an electric shock, why should you push them away using a non-conducting material?

## Answers

1. The metal wire is surrounded by another material.
2. When the bulb glows, electric current is passing through the object you connected.
3. A non-conducting material does not let current pass, so it keeps you safe from the electric shock.
