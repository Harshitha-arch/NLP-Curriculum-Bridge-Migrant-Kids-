## What you already know
You know that things can change size when they get hot or cold. A metal lid can feel tight on a jar, and it can loosen when it gets warm. In this lesson you will see how people use this change in size to hold things firmly together.

## Lesson
Some materials grow bigger when they are heated. This is called **expansion**. When they cool down, they become smaller again. This is called **contraction**. The **temperature** tells us how hot or cold something is.

People use expansion and contraction to make things hold tightly. Here are two examples.

**Example 1: Putting an iron rim on a wooden wheel**

A wooden wheel needs a strong iron ring around its edge, called the **rim**. The inside of the iron ring is a little smaller than the wooden wheel. So the ring will not slide on easily.

What do workers do? First, they heat the iron ring. The heat makes it expand, so it gets bigger. The hot ring now slides over the wooden wheel easily.

Next, they pour cold water on the iron ring. The ring cools and contracts, so it becomes smaller. Now it grips the wooden wheel very tightly.

**Example 2: Rivets joining steel plates**

A **rivet** is a small metal pin. Rivets are used to join two steel plates together.

First, a hot rivet is pushed through a hole in the plates. Then one end of the rivet is hammered to make a new head. This holds the rivet in place.

When the rivet cools, it contracts. This pulls the two plates tightly together.

**Remember:** Heating makes things expand. Cooling makes things contract. Engineers use this to make joints tight and strong.

## Key words
- **expand**: become bigger in size when heated
- **contract**: become smaller in size when cooled
- **temperature**: how hot or cold something is
- **diameter**: the distance straight across a circle, from one side to the other
- **rim**: the outer edge of a wheel
- **rivet**: a small metal pin used to join two pieces of metal
- **steel plates**: flat sheets of steel

## Practice questions
1. What happens to a metal when it is heated?
2. Why is the iron ring heated before it is put on the wooden wheel?
3. Why is cold water poured on the iron ring after it is fitted on the wheel?
4. How does a cooling rivet hold two steel plates together?
5. Why can a hot ring slide on a wheel when a cold ring cannot?

## Answers
1. When a metal is heated, it expands, which means it becomes bigger.
2. The iron ring is heated so that it expands and can slip easily over the wooden wheel.
3. The cold water makes the ring contract, so it becomes smaller and holds the wheel tightly.
4. When the hot rivet cools, it contracts and pulls the two steel plates tightly together.
5. A hot ring is bigger because it has expanded, so it slides over the wheel. A cold ring is smaller, so it cannot slide on easily.
