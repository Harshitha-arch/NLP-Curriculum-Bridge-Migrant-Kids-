## What you already know
You see changes every day. Milk turns into curd, and fruit becomes soft and sweet when it ripens. Some changes are good for us, and some are bad for the world around us.

## Lesson
Today we learn about two kinds of changes: **desirable** and **undesirable**.

Desirable means wanted. A desirable change is a change that is useful. It is not harmful to our **environment**. The environment is everything around us, like the air, water, land, plants and animals. We also want these changes to happen.

Here are some desirable changes:
- Fruit ripens. It becomes ready to eat.
- Plants grow.
- Food is cooked.
- Milk changes to curd.

Now let us look at the other kind. An undesirable change is a change that is harmful to our environment. We do not want these changes.

Here are some undesirable changes:
- Deforestation. This means cutting down forests.
- Fruit decays. It rots and goes bad.
- Iron rusts. It turns reddish and becomes weak.

Think about a fruit. When it ripens, we are happy because we can eat it. When it decays, we throw it away. Both are changes, but one is desirable and one is undesirable.

So, to decide if a change is desirable or undesirable, ask two questions. Is it useful? Is it harmful to our environment? If it is useful and not harmful, it is desirable. If it is harmful and not wanted, it is undesirable.

## Key words
- **desirable**: wanted and useful, not harmful
- **undesirable**: not wanted and harmful
- **environment**: everything around us, such as air, water, land, plants and animals
- **harmful**: causing damage or hurt
- **useful**: helpful for us
- **ripening**: becoming ready to eat, as fruit does
- **decaying**: rotting and going bad
- **deforestation**: cutting down forests
- **rusting**: iron turning reddish and weak over time
- **change**: when something becomes different

## Practice questions
1. What are desirable changes?
2. What are undesirable changes?
3. Name one desirable change and one undesirable change from the lesson.
4. Why is cooking food a desirable change?
5. A banana in your kitchen turns black and smells bad. Is this a desirable or an undesirable change? Give a reason.

## Answers
1. Desirable changes are changes that are useful, not harmful to our environment, and wanted by us.
2. Undesirable changes are changes that are harmful to our environment and not wanted by us.
3. Ripening of fruit is a desirable change, and rusting of iron is an undesirable change.
4. Cooking food is desirable because it is useful to us and is not harmful to the environment.
5. It is undesirable, because the banana is decaying, which is harmful and not wanted.

## Note for the teacher
This lesson is based only on the SCERT passage's definitions and its eight example changes, and it explains the terms simply. The passage does not explain why these changes happen, so that part is left out, and the word "deforestation" is explained in general terms since the passage does not define it.
