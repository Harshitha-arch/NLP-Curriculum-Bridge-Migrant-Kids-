## What you already know

You have seen that a push or a pull can make things move. In your earlier classes and your NCERT book, you learned that an object is in motion when its position changes.

## Lesson

Today we learn about push and pull.

Think about opening a door. You push or pull the door, and it moves. Think about kicking a football. Your foot pushes the ball, and the ball moves. Think about lifting your school bag. You pull it up, and the bag moves.

In each of these, there is motion. In each of these, there is a push or a pull. Some motions need a push. Some need a pull. Some need both.

So, when something moves, look for a push or a pull. A push or a pull is often the reason why the thing moves.

This lesson is short because the textbook passage is short. We will learn more about force and motion later.

## Key words

- **push**: to press or move something away from you
- **pull**: to draw something towards you
- **motion**: moving from one place to another

## Practice questions

1. Name two actions from the lesson where a push or pull is involved.
2. When you open a door, why is there motion?
3. Name one thing you move at home by pushing or pulling it.

## Answers

1. Opening a door and kicking a football are two actions where a push or pull is involved. (Lifting a school bag is another.)
2. There is motion when you open a door because you push or pull the door, and it moves.
3. Any everyday example is correct, such as pushing a chair or pulling a drawer open.

## Note for the teacher

The SCERT passage has one short introduction on push, pull and motion, and then a list of learning goals with no explanation. The goals on contact and non-contact forces, speed, uniform motion and calculations are left out because the passage gives no content for them. The "What you already know" section links to NCERT's work on motion and rest, which the child has probably studied.
