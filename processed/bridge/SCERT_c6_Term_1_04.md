## What you already know

You know that day changes into night and night changes into day. In NCERT you learned how to measure length with a metre scale, and now you will learn how we measure time.

## Lesson

Time keeps moving. Day changes into night, and night changes into day. The seasons also change. We know that time changes, but how do we measure that change?

We use clocks to measure time. You can read a clock face and note the time. This tells us the time of day.

Before clocks were common, people used other ways. In the earlier days, people used a sand clock and a sundial to measure time during the day. The shadow cast by a stick can be used to estimate time. A sand clock is a vessel with a small hole. It is filled with sand. The sand comes down through the hole, and this is used to estimate time.

You can also use your pulse to measure time roughly. Your pulse is the beat you feel in your wrist or neck. Count the number of pulses. This can tell you the time that has **elapsed**, which means the time that has passed.

Let us try an activity. Ask four or five friends to run a race from one end of the school to the other. Mark the starting point and the ending point. Count the time each friend takes. You can count your pulses, or you can count 1, 2, 3 in your head. Then check who is fast.

These pulse and sand methods are rough. For smaller **durations**, which means how long something lasts, we use an electronic clock, a **stopwatch**, and other instruments.

## Key words

- **time**: how long something lasts, or the order in which things happen
- **clock**: a device used to measure time
- **pulse**: the regular beat you can feel in your wrist or neck
- **elapsed**: time that has passed
- **sundial**: an old way to tell time during the day, using the shadow of a stick
- **shadow**: the dark shape made when something blocks light
- **sand clock**: an old clock in which sand flows through a small hole to show time passing
- **estimate**: to find a rough value, not an exact one
- **duration**: how long something lasts
- **stopwatch**: a clock used to measure small durations of time

## Practice questions

1. Name two tools that people used in the earlier days to measure time.
2. What does the word "elapsed" mean?
3. Why is counting your pulse a rough way to measure time?
4. In the race activity, what did you mark, and how can you check who is fast?
5. You need to know how long a short task takes, but you have no clock. What can you use, and what does it tell you?

## Answers

1. People used a sand clock and a sundial to measure time in the earlier days.
2. "Elapsed" means the time that has passed.
3. Counting your pulse is rough because it only gives an approximate idea of time, not an exact one.
4. You mark the starting point and the ending point, then count the time each friend takes to finish. The friend with the shortest time is the fastest.
5. You can count your pulses. This tells you the time that has elapsed, though only roughly.
