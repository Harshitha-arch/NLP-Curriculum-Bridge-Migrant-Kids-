## What you already know
You know that we need oxygen from the air to live, and that living things breathe. In this lesson you will see that plants need air too, and that both plants and animals use oxygen to get energy.

## Lesson
Plants and animals both need air to live. Here is how.

**Why living things need energy**
Every living thing needs energy. Energy helps the body move, grow and repair itself. Plants need energy to grow too.

**Respiration**
Respiration is the process that makes energy in the body. Oxygen is needed for it. Oxygen reacts with digested food and gives carbon dioxide, water vapour and energy. We can write it like this:

Food + Oxygen → Carbon dioxide + Water + Energy

**Respiration in animals**
When we breathe in, the oxygen in the air reacts with our digested food. The carbon dioxide made in the body goes into the blood. We breathe it out through the lungs.

Breathed-in air and breathed-out air have the same amount of nitrogen. Breathed-in air has more oxygen. Breathed-out air has more carbon dioxide and water vapour.

**Respiration in plants**
Plants also respire. They take in oxygen and give out carbon dioxide, just as animals do. Air goes in and out of the leaves through tiny holes called stomata.

**Photosynthesis: how plants make food**
Plants make their own food. This process is called photosynthesis. Carbon dioxide from the air and water from the soil react together in sunlight to make food. Most plants have a green pigment called chlorophyll. A pigment is a colouring substance. Chlorophyll is also used up in this process.

Here is the word equation:

Carbon dioxide + Water → Food + Oxygen (in sunlight, with chlorophyll)

Plants give out much more oxygen during photosynthesis than they use in respiration. So plants add oxygen to the air.

**Air and a burning candle**
Think about a burning candle. If you cover it with a glass cover, it goes out after a while. The candle uses the oxygen in the small amount of air inside the cover. When all that oxygen is used up, the candle goes out. A candle in open air keeps burning because fresh air keeps coming to it.

**Living in water**
Water in ponds, lakes, rivers and seas has some air dissolved in it. Dissolved means mixed in so well that we cannot see it. This air has oxygen. Fish use their gills to take in this oxygen from water. Frogs breathe through their skin.

## Key words
- **respiration**: the process in which oxygen reacts with food in the body to give energy
- **photosynthesis**: the process by which plants make food using sunlight
- **chlorophyll**: the green colouring in most plants that is used in photosynthesis
- **pigment**: a colouring substance
- **stomata**: tiny holes on leaves through which air goes in and out
- **energy**: the power that living things need to move, grow and repair
- **inhale**: to breathe in
- **exhale**: to breathe out
- **dissolved**: mixed into water so well that it cannot be seen
- **gills**: body parts that fish use to take in oxygen from water

## Practice questions
1. What gas do plants take in during respiration?
2. What are the tiny holes on leaves called, and what do they help with?
3. What does a plant need to make food during photosynthesis?
4. Why does a covered candle go out?
5. Why do fish need air dissolved in water?

## Answers
1. Plants take in oxygen during respiration.
2. The tiny holes on leaves are called stomata. Air goes in and out of the leaves through them.
3. Plants need carbon dioxide from the air, water from the soil, and sunlight to make food.
4. The candle uses up the oxygen in the air inside the cover. When all the oxygen is used up, the candle goes out.
5. Fish need the oxygen dissolved in water to breathe. They take it in through their gills.
