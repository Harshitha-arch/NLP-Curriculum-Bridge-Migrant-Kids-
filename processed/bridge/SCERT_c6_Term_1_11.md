## What you already know
When you pick up an egg, you feel how soft or hard it is and hold it gently so it does not break. You also know that a lift or a washing machine works by itself once you press a button. This is the idea behind a robot.

## Lesson
Today we learn about robots.

A robot is an **automatic machine**. Automatic means it can do a job by itself, after it has been set up. Some robots do jobs that are the same again and again. These are called **repetitive** jobs. Robots can do these jobs faster and more accurately than people. Accurate means doing the job exactly right.

Robots can also do work that is dangerous for people. They can handle dangerous materials. Robots can also explore distant planets, which are very far away from us.

How does a robot know how hard to hold something? Robots have **sensors**. A sensor is a part that lets a machine sense things around it. Sensors give the robot a sense of touch. With this touch, the robot can judge how to grip an egg or a heavy bag of luggage. It holds the egg gently and the heavy bag firmly.

Robots also have computers built inside them. These computers send and receive information using **radio waves**. Radio waves are waves that carry information without wires.

Now let us think about thinking. **Artificial intelligence** is an attempt to make computer programs that think like the human brain. A **program** is a set of instructions that tells a computer what to do. Scientists have not yet achieved this fully. But some computers can be programmed to recognise faces in a crowd. Robots can also play complex games, such as chess, better than human beings.

But can a robot know that it is thinking? Humans are **conscious**. This means we know that we are thinking. We do not yet know how consciousness works. So we do not know if robots can ever be conscious.

Some robots are very tiny. A **nanobot** is a robot made very small, almost **microscopic**. Microscopic means so small that you need a microscope to see it. Nanobots can be put into very small spaces to do a job. In the future, nanobots might be placed in the blood stream to do surgery. Surgery is an operation done inside the body. Nanobots might do surgery that is too delicate for normal surgery. A nanobot might even find **cancer cells** and destroy them without touching the healthy cells next to them.

## Key words
- **Automatic**: working by itself after it is set up
- **Sensor**: a part that helps a machine sense things around it
- **Radio waves**: waves that carry information without wires
- **Artificial intelligence**: an attempt to make computers think like the human brain
- **Program**: a set of instructions that tells a computer what to do
- **Conscious**: knowing that you are thinking
- **Nanobot**: a robot scaled down to a microscopic size
- **Microscopic**: so small that you need a microscope to see it

## Practice questions
1. What is a robot?
2. What do sensors give a robot?
3. Why can a robot hold an egg gently and a heavy bag firmly?
4. What is artificial intelligence?
5. Why might a nanobot be useful in the body, and why must it not touch healthy cells?

## Answers
1. A robot is an automatic machine.
2. Sensors give a robot a sense of touch.
3. Its sensors help it judge how much grip to use for each thing.
4. Artificial intelligence is an attempt to make computer programs that think like human brains.
5. A nanobot might do very delicate surgery or destroy cancer cells, and it must not touch healthy cells so that it does not harm them.
