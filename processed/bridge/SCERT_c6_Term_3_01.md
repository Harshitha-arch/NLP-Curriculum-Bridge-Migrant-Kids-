## What you already know
You have probably seen magnets pull some objects, such as steel paper clips, while other objects are not pulled. This lesson starts from that idea and moves on to new things in the Tamil Nadu book.

## Lesson
Today we start the unit on **magnetism**. Magnetism is the force that a **magnet** has. You might have seen magnets. Have you ever enjoyed playing with them?

Our first activity uses a steel glass, a small magnet, a needle and a thread. Here is what to do:

1. Put a small magnet inside a steel glass.
2. Take a needle and pass a thread through its hole.
3. Press the thread with your finger near the hole of the needle.
4. Lift the glass up slowly.

Watch carefully. Does the needle stand straight up without touching the glass? Your teacher will do the same activity. Write down what you see. Then think about this question: **why does this happen?** We will find the answer as we learn more about magnets.

In this unit, we will learn to:

- know how magnets were discovered,
- find out which materials are **magnetic** and which are **non-magnetic**,
- tell apart the **north** and **south** poles of a magnet,
- list the **properties** (special features) of magnets,
- explain the **principle** (basic idea) behind the **Maglev train**.

Keep your notebook ready. Write one new word and its meaning every time we learn something new.

## Key words
- **magnet**: an object that can pull some materials towards it
- **magnetism**: the force a magnet has
- **magnetic material**: a material that a magnet pulls towards it
- **non-magnetic material**: a material that a magnet does not pull towards it
- **north pole**: one end of a magnet
- **south pole**: the other end of a magnet
- **properties**: special features of something
- **principle**: the basic idea behind how something works
- **Maglev train**: a type of train named in this unit
- **discovery**: finding something for the first time

## Practice questions
1. What is the name of the activity we do with the steel glass, magnet, needle and thread?
2. What do we need to observe in this activity?
3. What two kinds of materials will we learn to identify in this unit?
4. What two ends of a magnet will we learn to tell apart?
5. Have you ever seen a magnet pull an object? Name one thing you have seen it pull.

## Answers
1. The activity is lifting a steel glass with a magnet inside, while holding a needle with a thread.
2. We need to see whether the needle stands straight up without touching the glass.
3. We will identify magnetic materials and non-magnetic materials.
4. We will tell apart the north pole and the south pole of a magnet.
5. Students may name any object they have seen a magnet pull, such as a steel paper clip.

## Note for the teacher
This lesson is based on the SCERT passage's learning objectives and its needle-and-glass activity, with the magnetic and non-magnetic terms linked to the NCERT material. The SCERT passage does not explain the needle result, the poles, the magnet properties, the discovery of magnets or the Maglev train, so these are only named here and should be taught from the later pages of the textbook.
