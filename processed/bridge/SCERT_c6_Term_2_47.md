## What you already know

You know your body has eyes, a heart and lungs, and each one has its own job. A cell is a tiny living unit, and it also has parts, each with its own job.

## Lesson

Every living thing is made of tiny units called **cells**. A cell is so small that you need a microscope to see it.

A typical cell has three major parts:

1. An outer **cell membrane**. This is the thin outer covering of the cell.
2. A liquid **cytoplasm**. This is the jelly-like liquid inside the cell.
3. A **nucleus**. This is the part that sits inside the cell.

Inside the cell there are also many small structures called **organelles**. Think of organelles like the organs in your body. Your eyes, heart and lungs each do a special job. In the same way, each organelle is a special structure that does a special job.

These jobs are important. They are needed for the cell to work normally. Many different organelles lie inside one cell, and each one helps the cell to live and work.

So a cell is not just one simple blob. It has a clear structure, with an outer covering, a liquid inside, a nucleus, and many organelles that each do their own job.

## Key words

- **cell**: the tiny unit that all living things are made of
- **cell membrane**: the thin outer covering of a cell
- **cytoplasm**: the liquid inside a cell
- **nucleus**: the part inside a cell that sits in the middle of the cell
- **organelles**: small special structures inside a cell, each with its own job

## Practice questions

1. Name the three major parts of a typical cell.
2. What are organelles?
3. How are organelles like the organs of the body?
4. Why do the organelles in a cell matter?
5. Your heart and lungs each do a different job in your body. Why is it useful for the body to have many organs with different jobs?

## Answers

1. The three major parts of a typical cell are the outer cell membrane, the liquid cytoplasm and the nucleus.
2. Organelles are specialized structures that lie within the cell.
3. Organelles are like the body's organs, such as the eyes, heart and lungs, because each one has its own special function.
4. Organelles perform valuable functions that are necessary for the normal working of the cell.
5. Each organ does a different job, so the body can do many things well. In the same way, different organelles do different jobs, so the cell can work properly.
