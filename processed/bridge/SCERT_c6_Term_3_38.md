## What you already know

You eat rice, vegetables and dal, and you may also eat eggs or fish. Some living things make their own food, and others must eat to get food.

## Lesson

Every living thing needs food. Some living things can make their own food. Others must eat other living things to get food.

**Producers** are living things that make their own food. They do not need to eat other living things. Plants are producers. A plant makes its food by **photosynthesis**, using sunlight. Producers are also called **autotrophs**.

**Consumers** cannot make their own food, so they must eat other living things. All animals are consumers. Consumers are also called **heterotrophs**.

Consumers are grouped by what they eat:
- **Herbivores** eat plants. Cow, deer, goat and rat are herbivores.
- **Carnivores** eat other animals. Lion, tiger, frog and owl are carnivores.
- **Omnivores** eat both plants and animals. Humans, dogs and crows are omnivores.
- **Decomposers** are tiny living things, such as bacteria and fungi. They break down dead plants and animals. This puts useful materials back into the soil, and plants use them to grow.

A **food chain** shows who eats whom. Here is an example from a grassland:

Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Crow

The energy starts from the Sun. Plants use sunlight to make food, and the energy is stored in the plant. When a grasshopper eats grass, the energy moves from the grass to the grasshopper. When a frog eats the grasshopper, the energy moves to the frog. When a crow eats the frog, the energy moves to the crow.

Each step in a food chain is called a **trophic level**. Plants are at the first level. Animals that eat plants are **primary consumers**. Animals that eat primary consumers are **secondary consumers**. Animals that eat secondary consumers are **tertiary consumers**.

Energy is lost at each step. The passage says only about 10% of energy goes into new body cells, and that part is available to the next animal when it is eaten.

Most animals live on more than one food. A **food web** joins many food chains in one place. It shows how animals in an ecosystem are linked. For example, a rat eats grains, a snake eats the rat, and a peacock eats the snake. Food webs help us understand the feeding links in an ecosystem.

## Key words

- **Producer**: a living thing that makes its own food, such as a plant.
- **Consumer**: a living thing that eats other living things to get food.
- **Photosynthesis**: how plants make food using sunlight.
- **Herbivore**: an animal that eats only plants.
- **Carnivore**: an animal that eats other animals.
- **Omnivore**: an animal that eats both plants and animals.
- **Decomposer**: a tiny living thing that breaks down dead plants and animals.
- **Food chain**: the order in which living things eat one another.
- **Food web**: many food chains joined together.
- **Trophic level**: one step in a food chain.

## Practice questions

1. What is a producer? Give one example.
2. Which living things are called consumers?
3. In the food chain grass → grasshopper → frog → crow, what does the frog eat?
4. Why is a decomposer important for plants?
5. Think of your own meal. Is it from a plant, an animal, or both? What would you be called in terms of the groups in this lesson?

## Answers

1. A producer is a living thing that makes its own food. A plant is an example.
2. Living things that cannot make their own food and must eat other living things are called consumers. All animals are consumers.
3. The frog eats the grasshopper.
4. Decomposers break down dead plants and animals, and put useful materials back into the soil, which plants use to grow.
5. Answers will vary. A meal of rice and vegetables comes from plants, so you are an omnivore if you also eat fish or eggs, which come from animals.
