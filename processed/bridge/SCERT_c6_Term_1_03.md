# Mass

*Bridge pack · Tamil Nadu SCERT Class 6 Science · unit: Measurements · gap label: Partially Covered · reviewed by Aaryaveer Katoch*

## What you already know

You already know that matter is anything that takes up space and has mass, and that we measure mass in grams and kilograms. Today you will learn more about mass and how it is different from weight.

## Lesson

Think about a book and a sheet of paper. You know the book has more matter in it. This means the book has more **mass**. Mass is the measure of the amount of matter in an object.

The SI unit of mass is the **kilogram**. We write it as kg. Mass is measured in kilograms, and smaller masses are measured in grams.

Now let us talk about **weight**. Weight is the gravitational pull on matter. Gravitational pull is the pull of the Earth that pulls things down. On the Earth's surface, weight is directly proportional to mass. This means that when mass goes up, weight goes up too.

Try this. Hold a sheet of paper in one hand and a book in the other. Which hand feels the heaviness? It is the hand with the book. The book has more mass, so the Earth pulls it harder. Your hand needs more force to hold the book than the paper. The force you feel is called **heaviness**.

Now think about the Moon. The Moon's gravitational pull is one-sixth of the Earth's pull. So an object weighs six times lighter on the Moon than on the Earth. But the mass of the object stays the same. The amount of matter does not change. Only the weight changes.

Mass can be measured in different units:

- 1000 milligram = 1 gram
- 1000 gram = 1 kilogram
- 1000 kilogram = 1 tonne

Your own mass in grams would be a very big number. So we write it in kilograms. Very big masses are measured in tonnes, also called metric tonnes.

In laboratories, scientists use electronic balances to weigh chemicals. This makes sure the measurement is exact. Electronic balances are also used to weigh food, grocery items and jewellery.

## Key words

- **Mass**: the amount of matter in an object
- **Matter**: anything that takes up space and has mass
- **Kilogram (kg)**: the standard unit for measuring mass
- **Weight**: the pull of gravity on an object
- **Gravitational pull**: the pull of the Earth that pulls things down
- **Heaviness**: the force we feel when we lift or hold something
- **Tonne**: a unit for very large masses; 1000 kg = 1 tonne
- **Milligram**: a very small unit of mass; 1000 milligram = 1 gram
- **Electronic balance**: a machine that measures mass exactly

## Practice questions

1. What is mass?
2. What is the SI unit of mass, and how is it written?
3. Why does the book feel heavier than the sheet of paper in your hand?
4. Why does an object's weight change on the Moon, but its mass does not?
5. Your friend says, "Weighing 50 kilograms and 50 grams is the same." Is your friend right? Explain why or why not, using the idea of mass.

## Answers

1. Mass is the measure of the amount of matter in an object.
2. The SI unit of mass is the kilogram, written as kg.
3. The book has more mass, so the Earth pulls it harder. Your hand needs more force to hold it, and you feel more heaviness.
4. The Moon's gravitational pull is one-sixth of the Earth's, so weight is less there. The amount of matter in the object does not change, so its mass stays the same.
5. Your friend is wrong. 50 kg is 1000 times more than 50 g, because 1000 g = 1 kg. The masses are very different.
