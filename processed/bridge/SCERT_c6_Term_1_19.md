## What you already know
You have seen tea, turmeric powder, and other things sold in shops at home. You may have wondered whether everything in the packet is really the same as what is written on it.

## Lesson
Some things we buy in the market are not pure. Sometimes they are mixed with other things that are harmful and not wanted. This mixing is called **adulteration**.

Adulteration can happen on purpose. It can also happen by accident, because of carelessness or because food is not handled properly. Either way, the food is no longer safe.

We must be careful about the common mixed-in substances in the things we eat and drink. Any adulterated food is harmful when we eat it. It can be a **health hazard**, which means it can make us ill.

An adulterated substance does not show the true properties of the original substance. In other words, the mixed food does not have the features it should have.

Here is an example. Used tea leaves are sometimes added to tea as an adulterant. An **adulterant** is the unwanted substance that is mixed in. Used tea leaves have already been brewed, so they are not fresh tea. They are added to tea that is sold as fresh.

Another example is turmeric powder. Turmeric powder is sometimes mixed with a bright yellow **chemical**. A chemical is a substance that is made or used in a process. This chemical is **poisonous**, which means it can harm or kill us if we eat it.

So, when you buy food, remember that it may be mixed with something harmful. Food should be safe and pure.

## Key words
- **Adulteration**: mixing harmful or unwanted things into something we buy to eat or drink
- **Adulterant**: the unwanted thing that is mixed in
- **Harmful**: able to hurt us
- **Substance**: any material or thing
- **Consumable**: something that we eat or drink
- **Health hazard**: something that can make our body ill
- **Carelessness**: not being careful
- **Handling**: how something is looked after, moved, and kept
- **Properties**: the features of a substance, such as its colour, taste, or smell
- **Poisonous**: harmful, or deadly, if eaten or drunk

## Practice questions
1. What is adulteration?
2. Name one adulterant that is sometimes added to tea.
3. Why is adulterated food harmful?
4. Why does the bright yellow chemical in turmeric powder make the powder dangerous?
5. Adulteration can happen because of carelessness. How might careless handling of food lead to adulteration?

## Answers
1. Adulteration is mixing harmful or unwanted substances into things we buy to eat or drink.
2. Used tea leaves are sometimes added to tea as an adulterant.
3. Adulterated food is harmful to eat, and it can be a health hazard.
4. The bright yellow chemical is poisonous, so it can harm us if we eat turmeric powder that contains it.
5. Food can get adulterated because of carelessness or poor handling, which means it can be mixed or spoiled without anyone meaning to do it.
