## What you already know

You know that ice melts into water and water turns into vapour when it is heated. In both changes, the water is still water. Chemistry asks a different question: what happens when a material turns into something new?

## Lesson

Think about a stick. If you break it into two pieces, you still have wood. The shape has changed, but the material is the same. This is a **physical change**. In a physical change, only the shape, size or volume changes. The material can also change its state, for example from liquid to gas, but it stays the same substance.

Ice melting into water is also a physical change. Water boiling into vapour is another one. The water is still water.

Now think about a paper. If you cut it into two, both pieces are still paper. But if you burn it, the paper is gone. Only ash and smoke are left. This is a **chemical change**. In a chemical change, the substance itself changes into something new.

Here is an easy way to remember it. In a physical change, the material is still the same material. In a chemical change, it becomes a different material.

Chemical changes happen all around us, and they are useful. Let us look at one example from your kitchen. Idli batter becomes soft because of a chemical change called **fermentation**. During fermentation, tiny living things called **bacteria** change the batter. This is why idlis are soft and light.

Chemists also use a simple test. Add a pinch of turmeric powder to water and it turns yellow. Chemists call turmeric a **natural indicator**. You can add turmeric to soap water, tamarind extract or cleaning liquids at home. If the colour changes, it shows that the liquid is either an acid or a base.

Chemistry is the study of very small particles, such as **atoms** and **molecules**, and what happens when they combine. Water (H₂O) is made when hydrogen and oxygen combine. Common salt (NaCl) is made from sodium and chlorine. Almost everything around us is made of these tiny particles.

Chemical changes also help us make useful things. Soaps, fertilizers, plastics and cement are made by chemical changes in natural materials. In this unit, you will learn more about these everyday materials.

## Key words

- **Physical change**: a change in shape, size, volume or state, where the material stays the same.
- **Chemical change**: a change where the substance itself becomes something new.
- **Natural indicator**: a natural material, like turmeric, that changes colour to show whether a liquid is an acid or a base.
- **Acid**: a type of liquid that is tested with an indicator to find out what it is.
- **Base**: the other type of liquid that is tested with an indicator.
- **Fermentation**: a chemical change caused by bacteria, such as the change in idli batter.
- **Bacteria**: tiny living things that can change other materials.
- **Atom**: a very small particle that makes up all materials.
- **Molecule**: a group of atoms joined together.
- **Fertilizer**: a material that is added to soil to help plants grow.

## Practice questions

1. What is a physical change? Give one example from the lesson.
2. What is a chemical change? Give one example from the lesson.
3. Why is burning paper a chemical change, but cutting paper into two pieces is not?
4. What is the name of the chemical change that makes idli batter soft, and what causes it?
5. Ice melts into water. Is this a physical change or a chemical change? Explain why.

## Answers

1. A physical change changes only the shape, size, volume or state of a material, and the material stays the same. Cutting a paper into two pieces is one example.
2. A chemical change makes the substance itself change into something new. Burning paper, which leaves only ash and smoke, is one example.
3. Cutting paper leaves two pieces that are still paper, but burning it leaves ash and smoke, so the paper is gone.
4. The change is called fermentation, and bacteria cause it in the idli batter.
5. It is a physical change, because the ice changes its state but the material is still water.
