## What you already know
You have learnt that water gets polluted when trash and waste from homes and factories are dumped into rivers and lakes. Soil can also get polluted, in much the same way as water and air.

## Lesson
Soil is the top layer of the land where plants grow. Soil can get polluted too. Land or soil pollution happens when **toxic** chemicals change the natural balance in the soil. Toxic means poisonous. Natural balance means the usual mix of things in the soil that keeps it healthy.

Where does land pollution come from? There are four main sources.

First, **farming**. Farmers sometimes use too many chemical pesticides and fertilisers. Pesticides are chemicals that kill pests. Fertilisers are materials added to soil to help plants grow.

Second, **mining**. Mining means digging up metals and other materials from the ground.

Third, **factories**. Factories make **industrial waste**, which is the waste left over from making things.

Fourth, **our own homes**. Homes produce **solid waste**, such as plastics and broken electronics.

Why does this matter? Soil acts like a sponge. A sponge soaks up water, and soil soaks up things too. When it rains, the pollutants sink into the soil. This can harm animals, humans and plants.

Here is the danger for our food. If we grow plants to eat in polluted soil, these dangerous chemicals can get into our food.

How can we reduce land pollution?
1. First, try to reduce waste. Then **recycle** the rest. Recycling means using waste again to make something new.
2. Always use a waste bin. Never **litter**, which means throwing waste on the ground.
3. Do not burn waste. The ash mixes easily with soil.

## Key words
- **Toxic**: poisonous.
- **Pollution**: when harmful things make air, water or soil dirty and unsafe.
- **Pesticides**: chemicals used to kill pests.
- **Fertilisers**: materials added to soil to help plants grow.
- **Mining**: digging up metals and other materials from the ground.
- **Industrial waste**: waste left over from factories.
- **Solid waste**: waste from homes, such as plastics and broken electronics.
- **Recycle**: use waste again to make something new.

## Practice questions
1. What is land (soil) pollution?
2. Name the four main sources of land pollution.
3. What does it mean that soil acts like a sponge?
4. Why can polluted soil be a danger to us, even if we never eat soil?
5. Name the three ways to reduce land pollution. Which one should we do first?

## Answers
1. Land (soil) pollution happens when toxic chemicals change the natural balance in the soil.
2. The four sources are farming, mining, factories, and solid waste from our homes.
3. Soil soaks up things, just as a sponge soaks up water, so pollutants sink into it when it rains.
4. If we grow food plants in polluted soil, the dangerous chemicals can get into the food we eat.
5. The three ways are: reduce waste and then recycle the rest; use a waste bin and never litter; and do not burn waste. Reducing waste should come first.
