## What you already know

Think of a brick wall. It is built from many small bricks joined together. Your body and a tree are also built from small units, but these units are far too small to see with your eyes. These small units are called cells.

## Lesson

Today we learn about the cell. Every living thing is made up of cells. A cell is a small unit that living things are built from.

We cannot see most cells with our eyes. We need a microscope. A microscope is a tool that makes very small things look bigger. With a microscope, we can observe the structure of a cell. Structure means how the parts of something are arranged.

Cells have parts. These parts are called components. Each part has its own job in the cell. Your teacher will show you the parts of a cell.

Scientists sort cells into two main types.

The first type is **prokaryotic cells**. These cells do not have a true nucleus. A nucleus is a part inside a cell. A true nucleus is a clear, separate part. Bacteria and cyanobacteria are living things made of prokaryotic cells.

The second type is **eukaryotic cells**. These cells have a true nucleus. Plant cells and animal cells are eukaryotic cells.

Plant cells and animal cells are both eukaryotic, but they are not the same. They have structural differences. This means their parts are arranged differently. You will learn these differences as you study more.

Remember:
- All living things are made of cells.
- We use a microscope to see cells.
- Cells have parts, called components.
- Prokaryotic cells have no true nucleus.
- Eukaryotic cells have a true nucleus.

## Key words

- **Cell**: a small unit that living things are made of
- **Microscope**: a tool that makes very small things look bigger
- **Structure**: the way the parts of something are arranged
- **Component**: one of the parts that make up a cell
- **Nucleus**: a part inside a cell
- **True nucleus**: a clear, separate nucleus inside a cell
- **Prokaryotic cell**: a cell that has no true nucleus
- **Eukaryotic cell**: a cell that has a true nucleus
- **Bacteria**: tiny living things made of prokaryotic cells
- **Cyanobacteria**: living things made of prokaryotic cells

## Practice questions

1. What are all living things made up of?
2. What tool do we use to observe the structure of a cell?
3. What is the difference between a prokaryotic cell and a eukaryotic cell?
4. Name two living things that are made of prokaryotic cells.
5. Why is it useful to know that a brick wall is built from bricks, and that living things are built from cells?

## Answers

1. All living things are made up of cells.
2. We use a microscope to observe the structure of a cell.
3. A prokaryotic cell has no true nucleus. A eukaryotic cell has a true nucleus.
4. Bacteria and cyanobacteria are made of prokaryotic cells.
5. Like a wall is built from bricks, living things are built from cells, so cells help us understand how living things are built.
