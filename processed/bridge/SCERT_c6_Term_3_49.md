## What you already know

From the Curiosity book, you learned that trees give food and shelter to animals and birds. In daily life, we also use plants for food, cloth, and wood. This lesson shows how people group plants by the ways we use them.

## Lesson

We all use plants every day. Plants give us food, and they also give us many other things that we use. Scientists and people who study plants group them by how people use them. This is called classifying. Classifying means putting things into groups that go together.

The word **economic** means useful to people in their daily work and living. Plants have economic importance because people grow, collect, and sell them, and they use them to make things.

Here are six main groups of plants, based on their uses:

1. **Plants as food.** Many crop plants give us food.
2. **Spice yielding plants.** These plants give us spices.
3. **Medicinal plants.** These plants are used as medicine.
4. **Fibre yielding plants.** These plants give us fibre. Fibre is a thin thread-like material.
5. **Timber yielding plants.** These plants give us timber. Timber is wood used for building and making things.
6. **Ornamental plants.** These plants are grown to make a place look beautiful.

Look at the pictures in your book. Farmers are harvesting paddy. Harvesting means cutting and gathering a crop when it is ready. A woman is making rope from coir. A man is making a chair. A plate has neem leaves in it. Think about which group each plant belongs to.

Plants and animals depend on each other. Plants give food and shelter to animals. Animals and people use plants in many ways. This is why we must take care of plants.

## Key words

- **economic**: useful to people in their work and daily life
- **classify**: to put things into groups that go together
- **crop**: a plant grown by farmers for use
- **harvest**: to cut and gather a crop when it is ready
- **fibre**: a thin, thread-like material from some plants
- **timber**: wood used for building and making things
- **medicinal**: used as medicine
- **spice**: a plant part used to add taste to food
- **ornamental**: grown to make a place look beautiful
- **raw material**: the material used to make something

## Practice questions

1. Name the six groups of plants based on their uses.
2. What does it mean to classify plants?
3. What is harvesting, and what crop is harvested in the picture?
4. Why are plants said to have economic importance?
5. Look at your lunch today. Which group of plants does your food belong to?

## Answers

1. The six groups are plants as food, spice yielding plants, medicinal plants, fibre yielding plants, timber yielding plants, and ornamental plants.
2. Classifying means putting plants into groups that go together, based on how people use them.
3. Harvesting means cutting and gathering a crop when it is ready. The farmers in the picture are harvesting paddy.
4. Plants have economic importance because people grow, collect, and sell them and use them to make things in daily life.
5. Food from plants belongs to the group "plants as food."

## Note for the teacher

This lesson is based only on the SCERT passage's learning objectives, six plant groups, and four picture questions, with NCERT's point that trees give food and shelter to animals used as a link. The SCERT passage does not explain the groups or answer its picture questions (the source of coir, the uses of neem leaves, and the material of the chair), so those parts were left out and should be discussed with the child using the book's pictures.
