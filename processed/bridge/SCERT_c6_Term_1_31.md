## What you already know
From NCERT, you saw that plants have different stems, leaves and flowers, and that animals live in different places, such as trees, land and water. In a pond, you may have seen frog eggs as a jelly-like mass. Today we learn a name for all this variety and how living things are grouped.

## Lesson
Look around you. Plants and animals are not all the same. Each plant and each animal is unique, which means each one is special in its own way. The variety of living things is called **biodiversity**.

Biodiversity means the variety and differences among living organisms and the habitats in which they live. An **organism** is any living thing. A **habitat** is the place where a living thing lives.

Biodiversity is found in many ecosystems. An ecosystem is a place where living and non-living things are together. The passage gives deserts, forests, mountains, lakes, rivers and agricultural fields (farm land) as examples.

In each ecosystem, living creatures, including humans, form a **community**. They live together and interact, which means they affect one another. They also interact with other animals, plants, air, water and soil around them. The living things form a **biotic community**. The non-living things form an **abiotic community**.

Now let us look at how living things are built. All living things are made of small units called **cells**. All the functions of the body are carried out by these cells. Some organisms are made of only one cell. These are **unicellular** organisms. Unicellular organisms are usually **microscopic**, which means too small to be seen with the naked eye. Amoeba, paramecium and euglena are unicellular. Amoeba does all its life activities inside one cell. It moves with fingerlike projections called pseudopodia, which means "false feet."

Other organisms are made of many cells. These are **multicellular** organisms. In them, different groups of cells do different jobs. Their cells form tissues, organs and organ systems. Tigers, monkeys, humans, earthworms, snails, fish, frogs and pigeons are multicellular. We can see them with the naked eye.

Remember: unicellular means one cell, and multicellular means many cells.

## Key words
- **Biodiversity**: the variety of living things and the places where they live
- **Habitat**: the place where a living thing lives
- **Ecosystem**: a place where living and non-living things are together, such as a forest or a lake
- **Community**: living creatures in one place that live and interact together
- **Biotic**: made of living things
- **Abiotic**: made of non-living things
- **Cell**: the tiny unit that all living things are made of
- **Unicellular**: made of one cell
- **Multicellular**: made of many cells
- **Microscopic**: too small to see with the naked eye

## Practice questions
1. What is biodiversity?
2. Name two ecosystems given in the passage.
3. What is the difference between a unicellular and a multicellular organism?
4. Why do you think a tiger is called a multicellular organism, while an amoeba is called unicellular?
5. Your school has a pond, a garden and a playground. Which of these is a habitat for frogs, and why?

## Answers
1. Biodiversity is the variety and variability among living organisms and the habitats in which they live.
2. Any two of: deserts, forests, mountains, lakes, rivers and agricultural fields.
3. A unicellular organism is made of one cell, while a multicellular organism is made of many cells.
4. A tiger is made of many cells, while an amoeba is made of only one cell.
5. The pond is a habitat for frogs, because frogs live in water and on land near it.
