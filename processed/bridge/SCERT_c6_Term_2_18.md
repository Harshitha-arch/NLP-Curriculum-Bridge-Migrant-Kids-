## What you already know

You may have used a torch, a clock, or a toy that runs on batteries. Those batteries are made of cells, and cells help make electricity that makes the torch light up or the toy move.

## Lesson

Today we learn about a **cell**. A cell is a device. A device is a thing made to do a job.

A cell changes **chemical energy** into **electrical energy**. Chemical energy is energy stored inside substances. Electrical energy is the energy that makes electric current flow.

How is a cell made? Three parts are needed:

First, we need a **chemical solution**. This is a liquid made by mixing a substance into water. This solution is called the **electrolyte**. In the solution, there are tiny charged particles called **ions**. Some are positive and some are negative.

Second, we need two different metal plates. These plates are put into the electrolyte. The plates are called **electrodes**.

Third, chemical reactions happen. A chemical reaction is a change in which new substances are formed. Because of these reactions, one electrode gets a positive charge. The other electrode gets a negative charge. This difference in charge makes electric current flow. The flow is continuous, which means it does not stop suddenly.

Cells are sorted into two types. The sorting depends on how steady the flow of current is. The two types are **primary cells** and **secondary cells**.

Remember: a cell turns chemical energy into electrical energy. It has an electrolyte and two electrodes. It gives a continuous flow of electric current.

## Key words

- **device**: a thing made to do a particular job
- **chemical energy**: energy stored inside substances
- **electrical energy**: energy that makes electric current flow
- **electrolyte**: a chemical solution (liquid) that has charged particles in it
- **ions**: tiny particles that have an electric charge, either positive or negative
- **electrodes**: the metal plates placed in the electrolyte
- **chemical reaction**: a change in which new substances are formed
- **electric current**: a flow of electricity through a wire or path
- **primary cells**: one of the two types of cells, sorted by how the current flows
- **secondary cells**: the other type of cell, sorted by how the current flows

## Practice questions

1. What is a cell?
2. What are the two metal plates in a cell called?
3. What is the liquid in a cell called?
4. Why does one electrode get a positive charge and the other a negative charge?
5. Think of a battery in a toy. What energy change happens inside the battery to make the toy work?

## Answers

1. A cell is a device that converts chemical energy into electrical energy.
2. The two metal plates are called electrodes.
3. The liquid is called the electrolyte, which is a chemical solution with ions in it.
4. Chemical reactions cause one electrode to get a positive charge and the other to get a negative charge.
5. Chemical energy inside the battery changes into electrical energy, which makes the toy work.
