## What you already know
You have seen many powders, such as sugar, salt and flour. Plaster of Paris is also a powder, and it is white. This lesson tells you what kind of powder it is.

## Lesson
Today we learn about **Plaster of Paris**.

Plaster of Paris is a **fine white powder**. "Fine" means the powder is made of very small, soft grains.

Scientists give every substance a **chemical name**. A chemical name tells us exactly which substance it is. The chemical name of Plaster of Paris is **calcium sulphate hemihydrate**. This is a long name, so we say it slowly: cal-ci-um sul-phate hem-i-hy-drate.

Plaster of Paris is **widely used for industrial purposes**. "Industrial" means connected with factories and industries. So this powder is used in many factories, not only in one place.

Remember these facts:
- Plaster of Paris is a fine white powder.
- Its chemical name is calcium sulphate hemihydrate.
- It is widely used for industrial purposes.

## Key words
- **fine**: very small and soft, like grains of flour
- **powder**: a solid made of many tiny loose grains
- **chemical name**: the name that tells exactly which substance it is
- **calcium sulphate hemihydrate**: the chemical name of Plaster of Paris
- **industrial**: connected with factories and industries
- **industrial purposes**: uses in factories and industries

## Practice questions
1. What does Plaster of Paris look like?
2. What is the chemical name of Plaster of Paris?
3. For what purposes is Plaster of Paris widely used?
4. Why do scientists give a substance a chemical name?

## Answers
1. Plaster of Paris is a fine white powder.
2. Its chemical name is calcium sulphate hemihydrate.
3. It is widely used for industrial purposes, which means in factories and industries.
4. A chemical name tells us exactly which substance it is.
