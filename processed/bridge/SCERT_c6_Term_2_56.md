## What you already know
Think about a school sports day. Many students work together, and each one has a job, so the whole day runs well. Your own body works in a similar way: many parts work together to keep you alive and moving.

## Lesson
Your body is made of many small parts. Some parts are very small. Others are big. Each part has its own job.

First, we have **tissues**. Tissues are the material that organs are made from. Next, tissues join together to make **organs**. An organ is a part of the body that does one main job.

Organs do not work alone. Many organs join together to do one job. A group of organs that works together to do one particular job is called an **organ system**.

Why is this useful? When organs are organised into systems, the body can do many activities more efficiently. Efficiently means doing a job well, without wasting effort.

Your body has **eight major organ systems**. They are:

1. Skeletal System
2. Muscular System
3. Digestive System
4. Respiratory System
5. Circulatory System
6. Nervous System
7. Endocrine System
8. Excretory System

Each system has its own structure and its own function. Structure means how something is built. Function means the job it does.

Some of these systems help with important **life processes**. These are work that keeps a living thing alive. This lesson names four of them:

- **Digestion**: breaking down food
- **Absorption**: taking in what the body needs
- **Respiration**: breathing and using air in the body
- **Excretion**: getting rid of waste

The systems of the body must also work together. This is called **coordination**. Your body systems work together so that you can move, eat, breathe and think all at the same time.

Remember this: tissues make organs, organs make organ systems, and organ systems keep the body working.

## Key words
- **Tissue**: the material that organs are made from
- **Organ**: a part of the body that does one main job
- **Organ system**: a group of organs that work together to do one particular job
- **Organism**: a living thing
- **Digestion**: breaking down food
- **Absorption**: taking in the things the body needs
- **Respiration**: breathing, and using air in the body
- **Excretion**: getting rid of waste from the body
- **Coordination**: working together smoothly
- **Function**: the job that a part of the body does

## Practice questions
1. How many major organ systems does the human body have?
2. Name any two organ systems from the lesson.
3. What is an organ system?
4. Why does it help an organism when its organs are organised into systems?
5. Your body needs to take in air, and it also needs to get rid of waste. Name the two life processes from the lesson that do these jobs.

## Answers
1. The human body has eight major organ systems.
2. Any two of these: Skeletal System, Muscular System, Digestive System, Respiratory System, Circulatory System, Nervous System, Endocrine System, Excretory System.
3. An organ system is a group of organs that work together to do one particular job.
4. Organised organs help the organism perform various activities more efficiently.
5. Taking in air is respiration, and getting rid of waste is excretion.
