## What you already know
Have you ever found a tight jar lid or pen cap hard to open? Your mother may have run the lid under hot water, and it became easier to turn.

## Lesson
Sam could not open a tight jar. His uncle told him to pour hot water on the lid. Sam did this, and the jar opened easily.

Why does this work? To understand, we need to know what things are made of. Every substance is made up of tiny parts called **molecules**. We cannot see them with our eyes.

The molecules in any object are always moving a little. This movement is called **vibration**, which means shaking back and forth. When one molecule vibrates, it passes the vibration to the next molecule. This is how heat flows.

Heat flows from a hotter place to a cooler place. The **temperature** of an object tells us how hot or cold it is, and it decides which way the heat will flow.

When a substance is heated, its molecules move faster. They spread apart and take up more space. So the substance gets bigger. This is called **expansion**. Most substances expand when they are heated.

When a substance cools, it usually gets smaller. This is called **contraction**. The change in length, area or volume is directly related to the change in temperature. A bigger change in temperature gives a bigger change in size.

Hot water warms the metal lid of the jar. The lid expands a little and becomes looser on the jar, so it turns more easily.

Now look at a nail. Hammer a nail into a tin can, then take it out. Heat the nail over a candle, in hot water, or over a stove. Try to put it back into the hole. You will find that it is hard to put in. The heated nail is bigger, so it does not fit the hole.

This shows that heat expands solids. A **solid** is something that keeps its own shape, like a nail or a lid. In a solid, the molecules move faster when heated, spread apart and take up more space.

The expansion of a substance on heating is called **thermal expansion**. Your teacher may show you an activity called linear expansion, using a cycle spoke and a bulb.

## Key words
- **Expansion**: getting bigger in size
- **Molecule**: a tiny part of a substance that we cannot see
- **Vibration**: quick shaking or moving back and forth
- **Solid**: a thing that keeps its own shape, like a nail
- **Heat**: something that flows from a hotter place to a cooler place
- **Contraction**: getting smaller when cooled
- **Thermal expansion**: the growth of a substance when it is heated
- **Temperature**: how hot or cold something is

## Practice questions
1. What happens to the molecules of a substance when it is heated?
2. Why was the nail hard to put back into the hole after it was heated?
3. What is thermal expansion?
4. Why does pouring hot water on a tight jar lid help open it?
5. Why might warm water help a tight pen cap open?

## Answers
1. When a substance is heated, its molecules move faster, spread apart and take up more space.
2. The heated nail became bigger, so it did not fit into the hole easily.
3. Thermal expansion is the expansion of a substance when it is heated.
4. The hot water warms the lid, so the lid expands and becomes looser on the jar.
5. Warm water heats the cap, so it expands a little and loosens, just like the jar lid.

## Note for the teacher
This lesson is based on the SCERT passage on expansion in solids, using the jar lid, the nail in the tin can, the molecule explanation and thermal expansion. The passage gives the set-up for the linear expansion activity but not its result, so the result is left out, and the explanation of why the lid loosens is an inference from the passage's statement that substances expand when heated.
