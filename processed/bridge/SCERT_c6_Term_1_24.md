## What you already know
You already know that plants have stems, leaves and flowers, and that some plants are herbs, shrubs or trees. In this lesson you will learn how the plant is built below and above the ground, and what each part does.

## Lesson
A plant is like your body. Your body has many organs. A plant also has organs: root, stem, leaves and flowers.

A flowering plant has two main parts:
1. **Root system**: the part under the ground.
2. **Shoot system**: the part above the ground. It has the stem, leaves, flowers and fruits.

**Roots**
The root fixes the plant firmly to the soil. It takes in water and minerals from the soil. Some plants store food in their roots. Carrot and beet are examples.

There are two kinds of root system:
- **Taproot system**: one main root grows straight down. Smaller side roots grow from it. Bean, mango and neem have this kind.
- **Fibrous root system**: many thin roots of almost the same size grow from the base of the stem. Grass, paddy and maize have this kind.

**Stems**
The stem supports the branches, leaves, flowers and fruits. It carries water and minerals from the roots up to the rest of the plant. It also carries food from the leaves to other parts. Sugarcane stores food in its stem.

The stem has **nodes**, the places where leaves join the stem. The part between two nodes is called an **internode**.

**Leaves**
A leaf is green and flat. It is joined to the stem at a node. The leaf has a stalk called the **petiole**. The flat part is the **lamina**. The green colour comes from a pigment called **chlorophyll**. Tiny openings called **stomata** are found on the lower side of the leaf.

Leaves make food for the plant. They also help in respiration and transpiration.

**A simple experiment**
Put a carrot in a glass of water with a few drops of blue ink. Leave it for two or three days. Then cut the carrot in half, lengthwise. The inside shows blue colour. This means water moved up through the carrot. So the root carries water.

Put a small balsam twig in water with red ink. The stem turns reddish. This shows the stem carries water upwards.

**Two ways to group plants**
Plants can be flowering or non-flowering. Sunflower is flowering. Riccia is non-flowering.
Plants can also be angiosperms, where seeds are inside a fruit, or gymnosperms, where seeds are not inside a fruit. Mango is an angiosperm. Cycas is a gymnosperm.

## Key words
- **Root system**: the part of the plant under the ground.
- **Shoot system**: the part of the plant above the ground.
- **Taproot**: one main root that grows straight down.
- **Fibrous root system**: a group of thin roots of similar size that grow from the base of the stem.
- **Node**: the place on the stem where a leaf joins.
- **Internode**: the part of the stem between two nodes.
- **Petiole**: the stalk of a leaf.
- **Lamina**: the flat, wide part of a leaf.
- **Stomata**: tiny openings on the lower side of a leaf.
- **Chlorophyll**: the green pigment in leaves.

## Practice questions
1. What are the two main parts of a flowering plant?
2. In the carrot and blue ink experiment, why does the carrot turn blue inside?
3. Name one plant that has a taproot system and one plant that has a fibrous root system.
4. Which part of the plant stores food in sugarcane?
5. A farmer sees that his maize plants have many thin roots of the same size. What kind of root system do they have?

## Answers
1. The two main parts are the root system and the shoot system.
2. The blue colour shows that water moved up through the carrot, so the root carries water.
3. Bean (or mango, or neem) has a taproot system. Grass (or paddy, or maize) has a fibrous root system.
4. The stem stores food in sugarcane.
5. Maize has a fibrous root system, because it has many thin roots of similar size.
