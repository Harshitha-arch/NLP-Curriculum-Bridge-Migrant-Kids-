## What you already know
You know that air, water, soil, and food come from nature, and that we should use them carefully without wasting them. Every day you also throw away peels, food scraps, and wrappers, and this lesson is about what happens to those things.

## Lesson
Waste is the things we throw away. Solid waste is the waste we make in our homes and schools. Scientists divide solid waste into two types: **biodegradable** waste and **non-biodegradable** waste.

**An experiment with soil**
Imagine two pots filled with garden soil. In the first pot, mix a banana peel, some vegetable peels, and a few tree leaves. In the second pot, mix a plastic carry bag, a sweet wrapper, and some metal foil. Watch both pots for two weeks. What do you notice? Is there a difference between the first pot and the second pot?

**Biodegradable waste**
Biodegradable waste can be broken down. Tiny living things such as bacteria, fungi, and small insects act on it. Examples are fruit and vegetable peels, leftover food, and garden waste like grass, leaves, weeds, and twigs. Oxygen, water, moisture, and heat help this breaking down, which is called **decomposition**. Decomposition breaks big, complex materials into simpler parts. The decomposed matter goes back into the soil. This feeds the soil with nutrients and minerals again.

**Non-biodegradable waste**
Non-biodegradable waste cannot be broken down into the soil by micro-organisms or natural processes. Examples are plastic, metal scraps, and aluminium cans and bottles. These things stay almost unchanged, even after thousands of years.

**What happens to mixed waste? Rani's story**
Rani eats a banana and a packet of chips after school. She throws the banana peel and the chips packet into the bin together. The waste mixes, the bin starts to smell, and the municipality collects the waste in a tractor. The tractor takes all the mixed waste to a big open dump.

Sometimes the open dump catches fire. When waste like the chips packet burns, unhealthy chemicals go into the air we breathe. The ash left behind pollutes the soil. When it rains, some dangerous chemicals go into the ground. Rain also collects in plastic garbage at the dump. These pools let mosquitoes breed, and mosquitoes can spread dengue and malaria.

Cows and dogs go into the open dump looking for food. Mixed waste smells like food, so animals can eat plastic by accident. This makes them sick.

Rani does not want animals to get sick or the town to become polluted. She decides to reduce pollution by disposing of waste properly. The passage asks us to learn about the 3 R's, and we will learn them next.

## Key words
- **Solid waste**: the waste we make that is solid, such as peels, food scraps, and wrappers.
- **Biodegradable**: able to be broken down by natural processes.
- **Non-biodegradable**: cannot be broken down by natural processes.
- **Micro-organisms**: tiny living things, such as bacteria and fungi, that are too small to see.
- **Decomposition**: the breaking down of waste into simpler parts.
- **Nutrients**: substances that help plants and living things grow.
- **Pollute**: to make air, water, or soil dirty and harmful.
- **Open dump**: a place where waste is left in the open.
- **Municipality**: the local government body that collects waste from houses.

## Practice questions
1. What are the two main types of solid waste?
2. Name two examples of biodegradable waste from the lesson.
3. Why does the plastic bag in the second pot not break down like the banana peel in the first pot?
4. What happens to rainwater when waste is left in an open dump?
5. Why do you think it helps to keep banana peels and plastic packets separate, based on Rani's story?

## Answers
1. The two main types of solid waste are biodegradable waste and non-biodegradable waste.
2. Fruit and vegetable peels, leftover food, and garden waste such as grass, leaves, weeds, and twigs are examples of biodegradable waste.
3. Micro-organisms and natural processes cannot break down plastic, so it stays in the soil, while the banana peel is broken down by micro-organisms.
4. Rainwater collects in plastic garbage at the dump, and these small pools let mosquitoes breed.
5. When the waste is mixed, it smells and can look like food, so animals may eat plastic by mistake and get sick.
