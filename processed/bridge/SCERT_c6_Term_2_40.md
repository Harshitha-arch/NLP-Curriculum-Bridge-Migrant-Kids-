## What you already know
You know that our body needs oxygen to live, and that the air around us is a mixture of gases. You also know that moving air is called wind. Today we will look at the many other ways people and nature use air.

## Lesson
Air is very useful. Let us see some of the ways it helps us.

**Air for breathing.** Plants and animals use air for breathing. We breathe in the air and take oxygen from it.

**Air for burning.** Fuels such as wood, coal, kerosene and LPG need air to burn. So air helps us cook and do many other things that need fire.

**Compressed air for tyres.** Air can be pressed into a small space. This is called compressed air. Compressed air is used to fill the tyres of many kinds of vehicles.

**Air and the water cycle.** Air plays an important role in the water cycle in nature. The water cycle is the way water moves around the Earth, between the sky, the land and the sea.

**The ozone layer.** Ozone is a gas. The ozone layer is found high up in the air around the Earth. It helps stop harmful rays from the sun from reaching the surface of the Earth.

**Oxygen cylinders in special cases.** Sometimes people need extra oxygen. This happens in three cases:
- a patient who has trouble breathing
- a mountaineer, a person who climbs a high mountain
- a diver, a person who goes deep into the sea

In these cases, oxygen is carried in gas cylinders, which are strong metal containers. The person breathes oxygen from the cylinder.

**Dry ice.** Carbon dioxide is a gas. When it is cooled to a very low temperature, it becomes solid without first becoming a liquid. This solid is called dry ice. Dry ice is a good refrigerating agent, which means it keeps things cold. Trucks and freight cars use dry ice to keep perishable items, such as meat and fish, cold while they are carried from one place to another. Perishable items are things that go bad quickly.

**Air and wind mills.** Moving air turns the blades of wind mills. A wind mill can draw water up by running a pump. It can also run flour mills, which grind grain into flour, and it can generate electricity.

So, air helps us breathe, burn fuel, move vehicles, bring rain, protect us from the sun, and make power.

## Key words
- **fuel**: a material that is burned to give heat or energy, such as wood, coal or LPG
- **compressed air**: air that is pressed into a smaller space
- **water cycle**: the way water moves around the Earth between the sky, the land and the sea
- **ozone layer**: a layer of ozone gas high up in the air around the Earth
- **radiation**: rays or energy that travel from the sun
- **oxygen cylinder**: a strong metal container that stores oxygen for breathing
- **dry ice**: solid carbon dioxide, which is very cold
- **refrigerating**: keeping things cold
- **perishable**: likely to go bad quickly
- **wind mill**: a machine whose blades are turned by moving air

## Practice questions
1. Name two fuels that need air to burn.
2. What is compressed air used for?
3. Why does the ozone layer matter to us?
4. Why do a diver and a mountaineer sometimes need oxygen cylinders?
5. How could dry ice be useful to a truck that carries fish?

## Answers
1. Wood, coal, kerosene and LPG need air to burn. (Any two of these.)
2. Compressed air is used to fill the tyres of many kinds of vehicles.
3. The ozone layer helps stop harmful rays from the sun from reaching the surface of the Earth.
4. A diver going deep into the sea and a mountaineer climbing a high mountain may need extra oxygen, so they use oxygen cylinders for breathing.
5. Dry ice is a good refrigerating agent, so it keeps perishable items like fish cold while they are being transported.

## Note for the teacher
This lesson is based on the SCERT "Uses of Air" passage, with only the basic idea of air and oxygen from the NCERT "Air" section. The SCERT passage gives the temperature for dry ice as "-570 C", which appears to be a typing error, so I left the number out; please check it in the textbook before teaching it. The passage also does not explain how air helps the water cycle, so I did not add details.
