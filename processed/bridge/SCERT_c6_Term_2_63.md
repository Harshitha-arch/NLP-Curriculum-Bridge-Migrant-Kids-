## What you already know
You use your senses every day. You see a friend's face, hear a bell in school, and feel a cool breeze on a hot day without thinking about it.

## Lesson
Your body has **sense organs**. A sense organ is a body part that helps you know what is around you. Think of sense organs as windows to the outside world.

There are five sense organs: **eyes, ears, nose, tongue and skin**.

Your eyes help you see. You can tell the colour, shape and size of things. You can tell if things are near or far, moving or still. Your eyelids and eyelashes keep your eyes safe.

Your ears help you hear and tell apart the sounds around you. Your ears also help you keep your balance when you walk, run or climb.

Your nose helps you smell, and your tongue helps you taste. Your eyes, ears, nose and tongue are the sense organs that help you see, hear, smell and taste.

Your skin is the largest sense organ because it covers your whole body. Skin helps you feel things by touch. Think about holding a cup of hot tea. Your skin tells you the cup is hot. When you touch a smooth stone or a rough one, your skin tells you the difference. Your skin can feel if something is hot or cold, smooth or rough, dry or wet, hard or soft.

Your skin also does other important work. It protects your body from **germs**, which are tiny living things that can make you ill. Skin forms a strong barrier against them. Skin also keeps your body moist and helps control your body **temperature**, which means how hot or cold your body is. Skin uses sunlight to make **vitamin D**, a substance your body needs.

Remember: we can see, hear, smell, taste and feel only through our sense organs.

## Key words
- **Sense organs**: body parts that help us know what is around us
- **Eyes**: sense organs that help us see
- **Ears**: sense organs that help us hear and keep our balance
- **Nose**: a sense organ that helps us smell
- **Tongue**: a sense organ that helps us taste
- **Skin**: the largest sense organ; it covers the whole body and helps us feel
- **Germs**: tiny living things that can make us ill
- **Temperature**: how hot or cold something is
- **Vitamin D**: a substance our body needs, made with the help of sunlight
- **Pathogens**: germs that cause disease

## Practice questions
1. Name the five sense organs.
2. What can your skin tell you when you touch something?
3. Apart from hearing, what else do your ears help you do?
4. How does your skin protect your body?
5. You put your hand in a bucket and feel that the water is cold. Which sense organ helped you know this?

## Answers
1. The five sense organs are eyes, ears, nose, tongue and skin.
2. Your skin tells you if a thing is hot or cold, smooth or rough, dry or wet, and hard or soft.
3. Your ears help you keep your balance when you walk, run or climb.
4. Your skin forms a barrier against germs and keeps them out of your body.
5. Your skin helped you know the water was cold.
