## What you already know

From your NCERT lessons, you know that we must use natural resources carefully and that throwing trash into water pollutes it. Solid waste management is about dealing with the waste we make every day so that it does not harm our environment.

## Lesson

Today we learn about **solid waste**. Solid waste is the dry waste we throw away, such as packets, bottles, paper and food scraps. It is our duty to make less waste and protect our environment.

**The 3 R's** help us do this:
- **Reduce** means making less waste. For example, write on both sides of a paper.
- **Reuse** means using a thing again and again instead of throwing it after one use. For example, use a cloth bag instead of plastic bags. Reuse glass bottles for other purposes.
- **Recycle** means using waste to make new products. For example, old clothes can be used to make paper.

**The waste pyramid** shows the best ways to manage solid waste. The first step is **avoid**. Before you buy anything, ask yourself, "Do I really need it?" For example, avoid buying packed foods.

Other ways to manage waste are:
- **Compost**: Microorganisms (tiny living things) break down organic waste, such as food and plant waste, into manure. This manure is a natural fertilizer. It makes the soil richer for plants.
- **Upcycling**: This means changing waste into something of better quality or with a new purpose. For example, used tyres can become chairs, and used PET bottles can become a pen stand.
- **Incineration**: This means burning waste in a special furnace called an incinerator. The great heat kills germs. It is used for things like expired medicines and blood.
- **Landfill**: This means dumping waste into pits and covering it with soil. Garbage in a landfill decomposes very slowly.

Every person makes waste. The average person in India makes 0.45 kg of waste every day. This may seem small, but many people together make a lot of waste. So reducing waste matters.

**Two steps to remember:**
1. Always reduce waste first.
2. Keep waste separate. Keep biodegradable waste (waste that breaks down) apart from non-biodegradable waste (waste that does not break down easily). When waste is mixed, the place becomes dirty. Clean waste can be reused or recycled easily.

Remember: reducing waste also reduces pollution.

## Key words

- **solid waste**: waste that is dry or solid, such as packets, bottles and paper.
- **recycle**: to use waste materials to make new products.
- **reuse**: to use a thing again and again instead of throwing it away after one use.
- **reduce**: to make less waste.
- **compost**: natural manure made when tiny living things break down organic waste.
- **upcycling**: changing waste into a new product of better quality or with a new purpose.
- **incineration**: burning solid waste in a special furnace called an incinerator.
- **landfill**: dumping waste into pits and covering it with soil.
- **biodegradable**: waste that can break down naturally.
- **hazardous waste**: waste that can harm people or the environment, such as used batteries and expired medicines.

## Practice questions

1. What do the 3 R's stand for?
2. What is composting?
3. Why should we keep biodegradable and non-biodegradable waste separate?
4. What is the difference between reusing and recycling?
5. You want to reduce the waste from your lunch box at school. What could you do?

## Answers

1. The 3 R's stand for Reduce, Reuse and Recycle.
2. Composting is the process in which tiny living things break down organic waste into manure, which is a natural fertilizer.
3. We keep them separate because mixing them makes the place dirty, while clean waste can be easily reused or recycled.
4. Reusing means using a thing again and again, while recycling means using waste to make new products.
5. You could avoid packed foods, carry food in a reusable box, and reuse the box instead of throwing it away.
