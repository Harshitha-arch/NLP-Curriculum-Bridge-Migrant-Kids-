## What you already know
You have seen that some living things are far too small to see with your eyes. Bacteria are living things like this, and they are so tiny that scientists need a microscope to study them.

## Lesson
Every living thing is made of cells. A cell is the smallest unit of life. Some living things are made of only one cell. These are called **unicellular** organisms. Bacteria are unicellular.

The cells of bacteria are called **prokaryotic cells**. "Pro" means before, and "karyo" refers to the nucleus. So a prokaryotic cell is a cell that does not have a true nucleus.

A **nucleus** is the control centre of a cell. Most cells have a nucleus with a covering around it. In a prokaryotic cell, the material that does the work of a nucleus is called the **nucleoid**. The nucleoid has no covering, which is called a **nuclear membrane**. So it is not a true nucleus.

Prokaryotic cells are very small. Their size ranges from 0.003 to 2.0 micrometres across. A **micrometre** is a very small unit of length. You need a microscope to see them.

Prokaryotic cells were the first form of life on Earth. Escherichia coli is an example of a bacterium with a prokaryotic cell. It is often called E. coli.

Look at the chart of cell sizes in your book. It goes from small to large: virus, bacterium, plant cell, egg of a human, egg of a frog, egg of a hen, and egg of an ostrich. Bacteria are among the smallest.

## Key words
- **Prokaryotic cell**: a cell that does not have a true nucleus.
- **Unicellular**: made of only one cell.
- **Bacteria**: tiny living things, each made of one cell.
- **Nucleus**: the control centre of a cell.
- **Nucleoid**: the part of a prokaryotic cell that works like a nucleus, but has no covering.
- **Nuclear membrane**: the covering around a true nucleus.
- **Diameter**: the distance across a round thing, from one edge to the other.
- **Micrometre**: a very small unit of length.
- **Escherichia coli**: a kind of bacterium with a prokaryotic cell.
- **Microscope**: an instrument that makes very small things look larger.

## Practice questions
1. What type of cell do bacteria have?
2. What is the difference between a nucleoid and a true nucleus?
3. Why do scientists say prokaryotic cells were the first form of life on Earth?
4. Bacteria are between 0.003 and 2.0 micrometres across. Why can't you see one bacterium with your naked eye?

## Answers
1. Bacteria have prokaryotic cells.
2. A true nucleus has a nuclear membrane around it, but a nucleoid has no nuclear membrane.
3. The passage says these cells were the first form of life on Earth.
4. Bacteria are too small to see with the naked eye. Their size is only a few thousandths to a few micrometres.
