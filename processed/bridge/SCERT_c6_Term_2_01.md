## What you already know

From your NCERT book, you learned that you cannot always trust your sense of touch to tell if something is hot or cold. Your hands can feel the same water as warm or cool, depending on what they were dipped in before.

## Lesson

Today we will learn two ways that heat can be made. Heat is a form of energy. We feel it as warmth.

**Way 1: Friction**

Try this. Rub your palms together fast for some time. Then hold them to your cheeks. How do they feel? Your palms feel warm.

When two surfaces rub against each other, we can make heat. Rubbing two surfaces together is called friction. Long ago, people rubbed two stones together to make a fire. The rubbing made heat, and the heat lit the fire.

**Way 2: Electricity**

Electric current is the flow of electricity through a wire or other material. A conductor is a material that lets electric current flow through it.

When electric current flows through a conductor, heat energy is produced. Many machines use this idea to make heat. A water heater, an iron box and an electric kettle all work this way.

**Remember**

- Rubbing two surfaces together can make heat.
- Electric current flowing through a conductor makes heat energy.

## Key words

- **heat**: a form of energy that we feel as warmth
- **friction**: the rubbing of two surfaces against each other
- **electric current**: the flow of electricity through a material
- **conductor**: a material that lets electric current flow through it
- **heat energy**: the energy that we feel as warmth

## Practice questions

1. Name two ways heat can be produced, as told in the lesson.
2. Why do your palms feel warm after you rub them together for some time?
3. Name one machine at home that makes heat by using electric current.

## Answers

1. Heat can be produced by rubbing two surfaces together (friction), and when electric current flows through a conductor.
2. Rubbing two surfaces together makes heat, so your palms feel warm.
3. An electric kettle, an iron box or a water heater makes heat from electric current.
