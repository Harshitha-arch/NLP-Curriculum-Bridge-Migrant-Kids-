## What you already know
You know that air is all around us and that we need air to breathe. You may also know that air is a mixture of gases, mostly nitrogen and oxygen.

## Lesson
Our Earth is wrapped in a huge layer of air. This layer is called the **atmosphere**. The atmosphere goes more than 800 km above the Earth's surface. **Gravity**, the force that pulls things towards the Earth, holds the atmosphere in place.

The atmosphere protects us from many harmful rays that come from the sun.

The air is thickest near the Earth's surface. As we go higher, the air becomes thinner. Higher up, gravity is weaker, so it cannot hold as much air.

The atmosphere has five layers. The layer closest to the Earth is the **troposphere**. This is the layer we live in. It goes up about 16 km. Wind moves in this layer. It also has **water vapour**, which is water in the form of a gas. Water vapour makes clouds. This layer is responsible for the weather we see.

A **weathercock** shows the direction in which the air is moving at a place.

Now let us see what air does for us. In an experiment, a candle was put in a glass jar that was sealed with water. The candle went out quickly. Something in the air was being used up by the flame. Later, scientists named this substance **oxygen**. When the candle burns, oxygen is changed mostly into **carbon dioxide**.

A scientist named Priestley then put a mouse in a sealed jar. The mouse died, because there was no oxygen. Next, he put a mint plant in the jar with a mouse. Both the plant and the mouse survived. The candle also kept burning. Animals use oxygen and give out carbon dioxide. Plants take in carbon dioxide and give out oxygen. So plants and animals help each other.

Jan Ingenhousz found that plants need sunlight for **photosynthesis**, the process plants use to make their food. He also found that plants clean air that has been spoiled by breathing animals or burning candles.

From these experiments we learn that air is a mixture of many gases, such as oxygen and carbon dioxide.

We can test for carbon dioxide. Blow air through lime water with a straw. After a few minutes, the lime water turns milky white. This shows that carbon dioxide is present in air.

## Key words
- **atmosphere**: the big layer of air around the Earth
- **gravity**: the force that pulls things towards the Earth
- **troposphere**: the layer of the atmosphere closest to the Earth, where we live
- **water vapour**: water in the form of a gas
- **weathercock**: a tool that shows the direction in which the air is moving
- **oxygen**: a gas in air that is needed for burning and for living things to survive
- **carbon dioxide**: a gas in air that is given out by burning and by animals
- **photosynthesis**: the process by which plants use sunlight

## Practice questions
1. What is the atmosphere?
2. How far does the atmosphere go above the Earth, and what holds it in place?
3. Why does the air become thinner as we go higher?
4. Which part of the atmosphere contains water vapour, and what does the water vapour make?
5. A mouse in a sealed jar died, but a mouse in a jar with a mint plant lived. Why?

## Answers
1. The atmosphere is the huge layer of air that surrounds the Earth.
2. It goes more than 800 km above the Earth's surface, and gravity holds it in place.
3. Higher up, gravity is weaker, so it cannot hold a large amount of air.
4. The troposphere contains water vapour, which makes clouds.
5. The mouse in the jar without a plant died because there was no oxygen. The plant gave out oxygen, so the mouse could survive.
