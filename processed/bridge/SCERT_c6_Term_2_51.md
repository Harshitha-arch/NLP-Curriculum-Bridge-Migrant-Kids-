## What you already know

Think of a house. It is built from many small bricks, and each brick is a separate piece. Living things are built in a similar way from very small units called cells.

## Lesson

Every plant and every animal is made of cells. A cell is a very small unit of a living thing.

Plant cells and animal cells are both **eukaryotic**. This word means the cell has a true nucleus. The **nucleus** is a part inside the cell, and it has its own covering, called a membrane. So we say the nucleus is membrane-bound.

**Plant cells** are different from animal cells in several ways:

- A plant cell is usually larger.
- It is hard.
- It has a **cell wall**. This is an extra layer outside the cell membrane.
- It has **chloroplasts**, which hold **chlorophyll**.
- It has large **vacuoles**. A vacuole is a part inside the cell.
- It has no **centrioles**. A centriole is a small part inside the cell.

**Animal cells** are different too:

- An animal cell is generally smaller than a plant cell.
- It is not as hard as a plant cell.
- It has no cell wall.
- It has no chloroplasts.
- It may have many small vacuoles.
- It has centrioles.

**Try this: look at onion cells.**
Take a thin, transparent peel from the inside of an onion leaf. Put it on a drop of water on a glass slide. Add a drop of iodine solution. Cover it with a cover slip. Look at it under a microscope. A microscope is a tool that helps us see very small things. You will see rectangular cells of the onion peel, and each cell has a nucleus.

**Shape of a cell.**
A cell is not flat like a round drawing. An animal cell is spherical, which means it is shaped like a ball. A drawing on paper shows only two sides, so it is called 2-dimensional. A ball shape can be seen from three sides, so it is called 3-dimensional.

## Key words

- **cell**: a very small unit of a living thing
- **eukaryotic**: a cell that has a true nucleus
- **nucleus**: a part inside the cell with its own covering
- **cell wall**: an extra outer layer found in plant cells
- **chloroplast**: a part of a plant cell that holds chlorophyll
- **chlorophyll**: the substance found in chloroplasts
- **vacuole**: a part inside a cell
- **centriole**: a small part inside a cell, found in animal cells
- **spherical**: shaped like a ball
- **microscope**: a tool that helps us see very small things

## Practice questions

1. Name one part that a plant cell has and an animal cell does not have.
2. What is the cell wall, and which cells have it?
3. Why does a plant cell look different from an animal cell under the microscope? (Think about size and hardness.)
4. What did you see in the onion peel under the microscope?
5. Why is an animal cell called spherical, and not round?

## Answers

1. A plant cell has a cell wall, but an animal cell does not.
2. The cell wall is an extra layer outside the cell membrane. Plant cells have it, and animal cells do not.
3. A plant cell is usually larger and harder than an animal cell, so it can look different under the microscope.
4. You can see rectangular cells of the onion peel, and each cell has a nucleus.
5. An animal cell is spherical, which means it is shaped like a ball, not a flat round shape.
