## What you already know
You have seen the Sun's heat dry chillies, and you feel warm when the Sun shines on you. This lesson explains what heat is and what it does inside things.

## Lesson
Heat is a kind of energy. Let us learn how it works.

Everything around us is made of tiny parts called **molecules**. We cannot see them with our naked eye. Even so, the molecules in every object are always moving. They shake back and forth. This shaking is called **vibration**.

When we heat an object, its molecules vibrate and move more. They move faster. When the molecules move faster, the **temperature** of the object goes up. Temperature tells us how hot or cold something is.

So we can say: heat is an energy that raises the temperature of a thing by making its molecules move faster.

Heat is not matter. Things made of matter take up space and have weight. Heat does not take up space, and it has no weight.

Heat is like light, sound and electricity. All of these are forms of energy.

We all know heat. We feel it on our body when the sun shines. We use heat to cook our food. We can also reduce heat. When we prepare fruit juice, we add ice cubes to it to reduce the heat.

## Key words
- **heat**: a form of energy that makes things hotter
- **energy**: the power to do work or make things change
- **molecule**: a tiny part that everything is made of
- **vibration**: a quick shaking back and forth
- **temperature**: how hot or cold something is
- **matter**: the stuff that things are made of

## Practice questions
1. What is heat?
2. What happens to the molecules in an object when we heat it?
3. Does heat have weight? Does it take up space?
4. Why do we add ice cubes while preparing fruit juice?

## Answers
1. Heat is an energy that raises the temperature of a thing by making its molecules move faster.
2. The molecules vibrate and move faster, so the temperature of the object goes up.
3. No. Heat does not have weight and does not take up space.
4. Adding ice cubes reduces the heat of the juice.

This lesson is based on the SCERT "Heat" passage. It builds on the Sun's heat in the NCERT "Nature's Treasures" passage, but does not reteach it; the passage also does not define molecule, vibration or temperature, so I gave simple meanings from its context, and it does not explain how ice cools the juice, so I did not add that. The passage ends before its section on sources of heat, so that part is left out.
