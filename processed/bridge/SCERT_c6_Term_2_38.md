## What you already know

You may have seen a candle or a matchstick flame, or seen someone blow on a fire to make it bigger. Fire needs air to keep going.

## Lesson

Today we learn about **burning**. When we burn things like a candle, paper, kerosene, coal, wood or cooking gas (LPG), something important happens. The burning needs **oxygen**. Oxygen is a part of the air around us. We cannot see it, but we need it.

The oxygen for burning comes from the air. So a burning thing needs a **continuous supply** of fresh air. "Continuous" means it never stops. If the fire keeps getting fresh air, it keeps burning.

Now think about what happens if we cut off the air. The burning thing does not get the oxygen it needs. So it stops burning. Let us test this.

**Activity: Oxygen is needed for burning**

1. Take two candles of the same size and height.
2. Use chalk to mark them as Candle 1 and Candle 2.
3. Light both candles.
4. Cover Candle 2 with a glass tumbler.
5. Watch both candles carefully.

Candle 1 has air all around it, so it keeps burning. Candle 2 is covered, so its air supply is cut off. From what we learned, Candle 2 should stop burning.

**Rockets are an example.** As a rocket goes high up into the **atmosphere** (the layer of air around the Earth), there is much less oxygen. So a rocket carries oxygen with its **fuel**. The fuel is what burns. The oxygen helps it burn.

**Two words to remember.** When a substance burns with oxygen and gives out a large amount of **light** and **heat**, this is called burning. When the process does not give out a **flame**, it is called **combustion**.

## Key words

- **Oxygen**: a part of air that is needed for burning.
- **Burning**: a process in which something uses oxygen and gives out a large amount of light and heat.
- **Combustion**: a process like burning that does not give out a flame.
- **Supply**: the amount of something that is brought to a place, such as fresh air to a fire.
- **Continuous**: going on without stopping.
- **Flame**: the bright, moving part of a fire that we can see.
- **Atmosphere**: the layer of air around the Earth.
- **Fuel**: a substance that burns to give energy, such as kerosene or wood.
- **LPG**: cooking gas.
- **Rocket**: a vehicle that goes high up into the sky.

## Practice questions

1. What does a burning substance need to keep burning?
2. What happens to a burning substance if we cut off its supply of fresh air?
3. Why does a rocket carry oxygen along with its fuel?
4. What is the difference between burning and combustion?
5. A candle is lit and covered with a glass tumbler. Why does the candle stop burning?

## Answers

1. A burning substance needs oxygen from the air, with a continuous supply of fresh air.
2. The substance does not get the oxygen it needs, so it stops burning.
3. As a rocket goes high in the atmosphere, there is much less oxygen, so the rocket carries oxygen with its fuel for burning.
4. Burning gives out light and heat with a flame. Combustion does not give out a flame.
5. The glass tumbler cuts off the fresh air supply, so the candle does not get the oxygen it needs.
