## What you already know
Think of a brick wall. Each brick is one small block, and many bricks together make the whole wall. In the same way, you use many small things every day to build bigger things, like words to make a sentence.

## Lesson
Today we learn about the **cell**. A cell is a very small building block.

Long ago, an Englishman named Robert Hooke was a scientist, a mathematician, and an inventor. A **scientist** studies how things work. A **mathematician** works with numbers. An **inventor** makes new tools or machines. Robert Hooke worked to improve the **microscope**. A microscope is a tool that helps us see very small things.

Now, close your eyes and imagine a brick wall. What is the basic building block of the wall? It is a single brick.

Your body is like that wall. It is made of basic building blocks, and these are called cells. Every living thing is made of cells. The cell is the basic structural unit of a living thing. "Structural" means it helps to build the body. The cell is also the basic functional unit. "Functional" means it does the work of living.

A cell is also **self-sufficient**. This means it can do all the important jobs that a living thing needs, on its own.

So, a cell is the smallest unit that can build and run a living thing. Each brick builds the wall, and each cell builds a living thing.

## Key words
- **scientist**: a person who studies how things work
- **mathematician**: a person who works with numbers
- **inventor**: a person who makes new tools or machines
- **microscope**: a tool that helps us see very small things
- **cell**: the basic building block of every living thing
- **organism**: any living thing
- **structural unit**: a part that helps build the body
- **functional unit**: a part that does the work of living
- **self-sufficient**: able to do its own important jobs without help

## Practice questions
1. Who was Robert Hooke, and what did he work to improve?
2. What is a cell?
3. Why can we compare a cell to a brick in a wall?
4. What does "self-sufficient" mean for a cell?
5. Name one other living thing, apart from yourself, that the lesson says is made of cells.

## Answers
1. Robert Hooke was an Englishman who was a scientist, a mathematician, and an inventor. He worked to improve the microscope.
2. A cell is the basic building block of a living thing, like a brick is the basic building block of a wall.
3. A brick is a single block that helps build a wall, and a cell is a single block that helps build a living thing.
4. It means a cell can carry out the important jobs of a living thing on its own.
5. Any living thing, such as a plant or an animal, is made of cells. (The lesson says every living organism is made of cells, so any plant or animal is a correct answer.)

## Note for the teacher
This lesson is based only on the short SCERT passage on the discovery of the cell. The passage's sentence about the microscope is cut off, and the two pictures it mentions are not included, so the lesson does not describe what Hooke saw.
