## What you already know

In NCERT, you found that your sense of touch cannot always tell you whether something is hot or cold. This lesson gives a better way to think about it: temperature. Heat energy always moves in one direction, and this lesson explains which way.

## Lesson

Think about water in two places. Water flows when there is a difference in the levels of water between the two places. It does not matter how much water is in each place. What matters is the difference in level.

Temperature works in a similar way. The temperature of an object is like its water level. Temperature decides the direction in which heat will flow.

Heat energy flows from a higher temperature to a lower temperature. So heat always goes from the hotter object to the colder one.

Heat is measured in calories. One calorie is the amount of heat needed to raise the temperature of one gram of water by one degree centigrade.

Now think about two objects, A and B. Let A be hotter than B. When you bring A and B into contact, heat flows from the hot body A to the cold body B. This continues until both bodies reach the same temperature.

Two objects are in **thermal contact** when they can exchange heat energy. When two objects in thermal contact stop changing each other's temperature, they are in **thermal equilibrium**. Their temperatures are then the same.

Here is an everyday example. Take a pot of milk from the refrigerator and put it on the kitchen table. The milk and the air around it are in thermal contact. After some time, the milk and the air have the same temperature. They are now in thermal equilibrium.

## Key words

- **Temperature**: how hot or cold an object is.
- **Heat energy**: energy that moves from a hotter object to a colder object.
- **Calorie**: a unit used to measure heat. One calorie raises one gram of water by one degree centigrade.
- **Thermal contact**: two objects can exchange heat energy.
- **Thermal equilibrium**: two objects in thermal contact have the same temperature and no longer change each other's temperature.

## Practice questions

1. What decides the direction in which heat energy will flow?
2. How much heat is needed to raise one gram of water by one degree centigrade?
3. You are holding a hot cup of coffee. Does heat energy move from your body to the coffee, or from the coffee to your body? Why?
4. What does it mean when two objects are in thermal equilibrium?
5. A glass of cold water is left on a table in a warm room. Which way does heat energy move, and why?

## Answers

1. The temperature decides the direction in which heat energy will flow. Heat flows from higher temperature to lower temperature.
2. One calorie is the amount of heat needed to raise one gram of water by one degree centigrade.
3. Heat moves from the coffee to your body, because the coffee is hotter than your body.
4. Thermal equilibrium means the two objects are in thermal contact and have the same temperature, so they no longer change each other's temperature.
5. Heat moves from the warm room into the cold water, because the room is at a higher temperature than the water.
