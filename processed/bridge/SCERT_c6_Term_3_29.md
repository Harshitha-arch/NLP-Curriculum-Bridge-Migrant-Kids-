## What you already know
You have seen cement walls, floors, and roads in your town. You may also have used chalk on a blackboard at school. In this lesson you will learn how a soft mineral called gypsum helps cement and how it is used to make things like chalk.

## Lesson
Gypsum is a mineral. A mineral is a natural material that comes from the ground. Gypsum is soft, and it is white or grey in colour.

The chemical name of gypsum is calcium sulphate dihydrate. This is a long scientific name. You only need to know that gypsum has this name.

Cement is a powder used in building. When cement is mixed with water, it becomes hard. This is called hardening. Gypsum is very important because it controls how fast cement hardens. When cement is made in a factory, a small amount of gypsum is added at the last grinding stage. This small amount stops the cement from setting too quickly.

Cement is used in three main ways:
- **Mortar** is a paste of cement and sand mixed with water. In houses, mortar holds building blocks together to make walls. It is also spread over walls as a coating, and used to lay floors.
- **Concrete** is a mixture of cement, sand and gravel. It is used to build houses, bridges and dams.

In 1824, Joseph Aspdin made a new cement called Portland cement. He burned finely ground chalk and clay in a kiln. A kiln is a very hot oven. The cement was called "Portland" because it looked like good building stone found in Portland, England.

Gypsum can also be heated to make plaster of paris. Plaster of paris has the chemical name CaSO4. 1/2H2O. Heating gypsum makes it partly dry. Plaster of paris has been known since ancient times. Its name comes from the gypsum found near Paris, the capital of France.

Plaster of paris is used:
- to make blackboard chalk
- in surgery, to set broken bones
- to make casts for statues and toys
- in the construction industry

Now try a simple test. Take three empty tumblers of the same size and label them A, B and C. Put two teaspoons of cement in each. Add one teaspoon of water to A, two teaspoons to B, and three teaspoons to C. After an hour, see which cement has set fastest. Touch each tumbler to see if it is warm or cool. This test shows that cement and water must be mixed in the right amount for the cement to set quickly.

## Key words
- **gypsum**: a soft, white or grey mineral that comes from the ground
- **mineral**: a natural material found in the ground
- **calcium sulphate dihydrate**: the chemical name of gypsum
- **hardening**: becoming hard and firm
- **setting**: becoming firm and fixed in shape
- **mortar**: a paste of cement, sand and water used to hold bricks or blocks together
- **concrete**: a mix of cement, sand and gravel used to build strong structures
- **kiln**: a very hot oven used to burn materials
- **plaster of paris**: a white powder made by heating gypsum, used for casts and chalk
- **fractured bones**: broken bones

## Practice questions
1. What is gypsum?
2. Why is a small amount of gypsum added to cement?
3. What is mortar, and where is it used in a house?
4. What is plaster of paris made from, and what is it used for?
5. Why would it be important to mix the right amount of water and cement?

## Answers
1. Gypsum is a soft, white or grey mineral that is found naturally.
2. Gypsum is added to control how fast cement hardens, so that it sets at the right speed.
3. Mortar is a paste of cement and sand mixed with water. In houses, it is used to hold building blocks together, to coat walls and to lay floors.
4. Plaster of paris is made by heating gypsum. It is used to make blackboard chalk, casts for statues and toys, and to set fractured bones.
5. The tumbler test shows that cement sets fast only when the right amount of water is mixed in, so the mix must be in a certain ratio.
