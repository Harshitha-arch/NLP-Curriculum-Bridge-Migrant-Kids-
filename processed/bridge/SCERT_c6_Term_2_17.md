## What you already know

You know that the Sun gives us energy, and you have seen solar panels on rooftops. Today you will learn where electricity comes from in our state and how each power station makes it.

## Lesson

Electricity does not come from a switch. It is made in places called **power stations**. Any device from which electricity is produced is called a **source of electricity**.

Ravi's grandfather switched on a light in the evening, and the whole house became bright. The electricity came from a power station far away. Let us learn how different power stations make electricity.

**Thermal power stations** use heat. Coal, diesel or gas is burnt to make steam. The steam turns a **turbine**, which is a wheel with blades that spins. The spinning turbine turns a coil of wire kept near an **electromagnet**. Because of **electromagnetic induction**, electricity is produced. Here heat energy changes into electrical energy.

**Hydel power stations** use water. Water flows from a **dam**, which is a wall built across a river. The flowing water turns the turbine. Here **kinetic energy**, the energy of moving things, changes into electrical energy. Hydel stations last a long time and cost little to run.

**Atomic power stations** use **nuclear energy**, a kind of energy used in these stations. It is used to boil water. The steam turns the turbine. Atomic power stations are also called nuclear power stations.

**Wind mills** use wind. The wind turns the turbine, and the moving air's kinetic energy changes into electrical energy.

**Solar panels** are also used to produce electricity.

Tamil Nadu has many power stations. Thermal stations are at Neyveli (Cuddalore) and Ennore (Thiruvallur). Hydel stations are at Mettur (Salem) and Papanasam (Tirunelveli). Atomic stations are at Kalpakkam (Kanchipuram) and Koodankulam (Tirunelveli). Wind mills are at Aralvaimozhi (Kanyakumari) and Kayatharu (Tirunelveli).

Some electricity is stored in **cells**. A **primary cell** cannot be recharged, so it can be used only once. Clocks, watches and toys use primary cells. A **secondary cell** can be recharged many times by passing electric current through it. A mobile phone's cell is the size of a hand. Cells used in cars and buses are large and heavy.

## Key words

- **source of electricity**: any device from which electricity is produced
- **power station**: a place where electricity is made
- **turbine**: a wheel with blades that spins when steam, water or wind pushes it
- **electromagnetic induction**: the way electricity is produced when a coil of wire turns near an electromagnet
- **kinetic energy**: the energy that moving things have
- **nuclear energy**: a kind of energy used in atomic power stations
- **dam**: a wall built across a river to hold back water
- **wind mill**: a machine that uses wind to turn a turbine and make electricity
- **primary cell**: a cell that cannot be recharged and is used only once
- **secondary cell**: a cell that can be recharged many times

## Practice questions

1. Which power station uses water flowing from a dam?
2. What does a turbine do in a power station?
3. Why do the cells in clocks and toys have to be thrown away after use, while mobile phone cells can be recharged?
4. Why does a hydel power station change kinetic energy into electrical energy?
5. Name one power station in Tamil Nadu that uses wind to make electricity.

## Answers

1. A hydel power station uses water flowing from a dam.
2. A turbine spins when steam, water or wind pushes it, and it helps make electricity.
3. Primary cells cannot be recharged, so they are used only once, while secondary cells can be recharged many times.
4. The flowing water moves the turbine, so the energy of moving water changes into electricity.
5. Aralvaimozhi in Kanyakumari District is a wind mill station.
