## What you already know
From your NCERT lessons, you have seen that plants have stems, leaves and flowers. You have also seen that animals such as crows and ants live on trees or in soil, and that they eat leaves, seeds and insects. In this lesson, you will learn how plants and animals depend on each other.

## Lesson
Plants and animals need each other. Animals rely on plants for their food and shelter. Shelter is a place where an animal lives and stays safe. This relationship helps animals, and it also helps plants.

Here is one example. A silkworm feeds on mulberry leaves and lives on the mulberry plant. Because of this link between a worm and a plant, we get silk. So this relationship is useful to people as well.

Insects and birds help plants make fruits and vegetables. Flowers have pollen grains. Pollen grains must move from one flower to another. Bright colours, smell and honey attract insects to flowers. When an insect goes from one flower to another, it carries pollen grains on its body. It leaves them on the next flower. This is called cross-pollination. Cross-pollination helps fruits and vegetables form.

Bees are the best pollinators. A pollinator is an insect or bird that carries pollen from flower to flower. Bees also give us honey. So insects and birds that help pollination must be protected. This helps us get the best yield, which means the best amount of crop.

Animals and birds also spread the seeds of many plants. Inside a bird's body, natural substances called digestive enzymes soften the hard outer layer of a seed. A softened seed can germinate more easily. To germinate means to start growing.

In coral reefs, plants and algae are food for many kinds of fish. Algae are small living things that are often plant-like and live in water. Fishing work is done in these areas.

If these natural links between animals and plants are harmed, the economy is also affected. The economy is how a country earns and uses money and work.

## Key words
- **Pollination**: moving pollen from one flower to another so that fruits and vegetables can form.
- **Pollen grains**: tiny parts from flowers that are carried from one flower to another.
- **Cross-pollination**: pollen carried from one flower to a different flower by insects or birds.
- **Pollinator**: an insect or bird that carries pollen from flower to flower.
- **Germinate**: to start growing from a seed.
- **Digestive enzymes**: natural substances in the body that help break down food.
- **Coral reef**: a rocky place in the sea where many plants, algae and fishes live.
- **Algae**: small living things, often plant-like, that live in water.
- **Shelter**: a place where an animal lives and stays safe.
- **Economy**: the way a country earns money and uses its work and resources.

## Practice questions
1. What do animals rely on plants for?
2. Which insect gives us honey and is the best pollinator?
3. Why does silk production depend on mulberry plants?
4. What do digestive enzymes in a bird's body do to a seed, and why does this help the seed?
5. Why should we protect bees and birds in our village?

## Answers
1. Animals rely on plants for their food and shelter.
2. Bees give us honey and are the best pollinators.
3. Silkworms feed on mulberry leaves and live on mulberry plants, so silk production depends on these plants.
4. The enzymes soften the protective layer of the seed, so it can germinate more easily.
5. Bees and birds help pollination, which helps fruits and vegetables form, so protecting them gives the best yield.
