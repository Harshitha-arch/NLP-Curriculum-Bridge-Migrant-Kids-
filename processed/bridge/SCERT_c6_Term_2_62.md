## What you already know
Think about how you know a pencil is in your hand, or how you close your eyes when the sun is bright. Your body sends messages very fast to help you do these things. The nervous system is the body part that carries these messages.

## Lesson
Your body needs a way to send messages. The **nervous system** does this job. It is made of tiny cells called **neurons**, also called nerve cells. Neurons work together as a network.

The nervous system has four main parts: the brain, the spinal cord, the sensory organs, and the nerves.

The **brain** is the main controlling part of your body. It is placed inside the **cranium**, which is your skull. Three layers of covering called **meninges** protect the brain. The brain controls and regulates activities all over the body. It is also the place where you feel awake and aware, and where you remember things. The passage says the brain can store as many as 100 million bits of information in a lifetime.

The brain has three regions. They are the fore brain, the mid brain, and the hind brain.

The **spinal cord** is a bundle of nerves. It starts from the brain and runs down through your backbone. It carries signals to the brain and from the brain to other parts of the body.

The nervous system does three main jobs:

1. **Sensory input**: Signals are carried from sensory receptors to the brain and spinal cord.
2. **Integration**: The brain and spinal cord understand these signals and decide what response to make.
3. **Motor output**: Signals are carried from the brain and spinal cord to **effectors**, such as muscle cells and gland cells. Effectors are the parts that act on the message.

Two important functions of the nervous system are **conduction** and **coordination**. Conduction means carrying messages. Coordination means different parts of the body working together in the right way.

## Key words
- **neuron**: a nerve cell, the basic unit of the nervous system
- **nervous system**: the body system that carries messages and controls the body
- **brain**: the main controlling organ of the body, inside the skull
- **spinal cord**: a bundle of nerves that runs from the brain down the backbone
- **nerve**: a bundle of fibres that carries messages
- **cranium**: the skull, the bony case that holds the brain
- **meninges**: three layers of covering that protect the brain
- **conduction**: carrying messages from one place to another
- **coordination**: different parts of the body working together
- **effector**: a muscle or gland that acts on a message from the brain or spinal cord

## Practice questions
1. What are the cells of the nervous system called?
2. Name the four main parts of the nervous system.
3. What are the three main jobs of the nervous system? Put them in order.
4. What does the spinal cord do?
5. Think about picking up a pencil. Which part of the nervous system's work would be the first step, and which part would move your hand?

## Answers
1. The cells of the nervous system are called neurons, or nerve cells.
2. The four main parts are the brain, the spinal cord, the sensory organs, and the nerves.
3. The three jobs are sensory input, integration, and motor output.
4. The spinal cord carries signals to and from the brain.
5. The first step is sensory input, when signals from sensory receptors are carried to the brain. Motor output, when signals reach muscles and move your hand, comes last.
