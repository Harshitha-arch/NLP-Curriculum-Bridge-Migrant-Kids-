## What you already know
You know that living things are different from non-living things, such as a pencil or a car. You also know that your body needs energy from food, such as carbohydrates and fats.

## Lesson
Every living thing is made of tiny parts. Each tiny part is called a **cell**. Inside each cell are smaller parts. Each part has its own job.

Let us start with the outside of the cell.

The **cell wall** surrounds and protects the cell. It makes the cell stiff and strong. Think of it as the protector of the cell.

Inside the cell wall is the **cell membrane**. It holds and protects the cell. It also controls what goes in and out of the cell. So it is called the gate of the cell.

Now look inside the cell. The space inside is filled with the **cytoplasm**. It is a watery, gel-like material. The other cell parts move around in it. So the cytoplasm is like an area of movement.

The **mitochondria** make and supply most of the energy for the cell. They are called the power house of the cell.

Some cells have **chloroplasts**. These are found in plant cells. Chloroplasts contain a green pigment called **chlorophyll**. Chlorophyll catches the energy of sunlight. The chloroplasts use this energy to make food for the cell. This process is called **photosynthesis**. So chloroplasts are the food producers of the cell.

**Vacuoles** store food, water and chemicals. They are like storage tanks.

The **nucleus** is the control centre of the cell. It acts like the brain. It controls all the activities of the cell.

The nucleus has its own covering, called the **nucleus membrane**. It surrounds and protects the nucleus. It controls the movement of materials in and out of the nucleus. So it is the gate of the nucleus.

Remember: each cell part has its own job, and all the parts work together to keep the cell alive.

## Key words
- **cell wall**: the outer covering that protects the cell and keeps it stiff and strong
- **cell membrane**: the thin layer that controls what goes in and out of the cell
- **cytoplasm**: the watery, gel-like material inside the cell where the other parts move
- **mitochondria**: the parts of the cell that make energy for the cell
- **chloroplasts**: the parts of a plant cell that catch sunlight and make food
- **chlorophyll**: the green colour in chloroplasts that catches sunlight
- **photosynthesis**: the way chloroplasts use sunlight to make food for the cell
- **vacuoles**: storage spaces in the cell that keep food, water and chemicals
- **nucleus**: the control centre of the cell that controls all its activities
- **nucleus membrane**: the covering around the nucleus that controls what goes in and out of it

## Practice questions
1. What is the cell wall's job?
2. Which cell part controls all the activities of the cell?
3. What does the cell membrane do?
4. Why are the mitochondria called the power house of the cell?
5. A water tank in a house stores water for later use. Which cell part does a similar job in the cell?

## Answers
1. The cell wall surrounds and protects the cell, and it makes the cell stiff and strong.
2. The nucleus is the control centre of the cell, and it controls all the cell activities.
3. The cell membrane holds and protects the cell, and it controls the movement of materials in and out of the cell.
4. The mitochondria produce and supply most of the energy for the cell, so they are like a power house.
5. Vacuoles store food, water and chemicals, so they are like storage tanks.
