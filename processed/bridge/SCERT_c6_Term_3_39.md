## What you already know

You have learned that water gets wasted and that plastic bags and trash can pollute rivers and lakes. You have also seen smoke from vehicles that makes the air dirty. This lesson looks at waste in a wider way: what waste is, the three kinds of waste, and why we must manage it.

## Lesson

Think about the things you throw away every day. Old notebooks, empty packets, broken toys and food scraps all become waste.

**What is waste?**
Waste is any substance or material that we have used and no longer want. It may be worn out, broken, or no longer useful. A torn school bag or a broken pencil box is waste.

**Everyone makes waste.**
Every person makes waste. This waste affects all ecosystems. An ecosystem is a community of living things and their surroundings. Yet most of us do not know where our garbage goes after it leaves our house.

**There are three types of waste.**

1. **Liquid waste:** This is waste in the form of liquid. It flows in our drains.
2. **Gas waste:** Some waste is in the form of gases that hide in the air. An example is the pollutants that come from factories. A pollutant is something that makes air, water or soil dirty.
3. **Solid waste:** This is garbage. We put it in our waste bins.

**Why do we need to manage waste?**
To protect our environment, we must do three things:
- reduce waste,
- manage waste properly,
- and maximise recycling. Recycling means using waste again so that less of it is thrown away.

Next time you put something in the bin, ask yourself: where will this go?

## Key words

- **waste**: any used material that is not wanted any more
- **substance**: any material or matter
- **ecosystem**: living things together with their surroundings
- **environment**: the air, water, land and living things around us
- **pollutant**: something that makes air, water or soil dirty
- **liquid waste**: waste that is in the form of a liquid, such as waste in drains
- **gas waste**: waste that is in the form of gas in the air
- **solid waste**: garbage that we put in waste bins
- **recycling**: using waste again so that less is thrown away
- **manage**: to look after and deal with something properly

## Practice questions

1. What is waste?
2. Name the three types of waste.
3. Why is it important to reduce waste and manage it properly?
4. Give one example of solid waste from your home.

## Answers

1. Waste is any substance or material that has been used and is no longer wanted, because it is worn out, broken or has no purpose.
2. The three types of waste are liquid waste, gas waste and solid waste.
3. It is important because it helps protect our environment.
4. Possible answer from the lesson: garbage that we put in our waste bin, such as food scraps or an empty packet.

## Note for the teacher

This lesson is based on the SCERT passage "Waste Management and Recycling", with the NCERT water and resources passages used only to link to what children already know. The SCERT passage does not explain how recycling works, what the methods are, or what "pollutant" means in detail, so these were left out or kept to simple meanings. The example for solid waste is a general one because the passage gives no specific example.
