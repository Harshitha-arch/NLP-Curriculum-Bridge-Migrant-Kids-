## What you already know

In the soil activity you used a magnifying lens to look closer at tiny things, and you learned that many small living things are too small to see with your naked eyes. Cells are even smaller, so you need a special tool to see them.

## Lesson

Think about a plant, a dog, or yourself. All of them are living things. The SCERT lesson tells us that **all living things are made of one or more cells**. That means your body is made of cells too.

Cells come in many types. But they all have some common features. Every cell has some features that are the same as in other cells.

Can you see a cell with your naked eye? No. Your **naked eye** means your eyes without any help. Cells are very small. They are **microscopic**, which means so small that you need a microscope to see them.

A **microscope** is a special scientific tool. It makes very small things look big so that we can study them. Today, scientists also use an **electron microscope** to magnify and observe cells. It is a stronger tool than the usual microscope.

The word "cell" has an interesting history. In the year 1665, a scientist named Hooke published a book called *Micrographia*. In this book he described the structure of tissue. He was the first to use the word "cell" for these structures. In Latin, the word "cellua" means a small chamber. The word fits well, because cells are tiny parts that look like small rooms.

The branch of science that studies cells is called **Cell Biology**. So, if you want to learn more about cells, you can study Cell Biology.

Remember: all living things are made of cells, and cells are too small to see without a microscope.

## Key words

- **cell**: the smallest unit of a living thing; living things are made of one or more cells
- **microscope**: a tool that makes very small things look big so we can see them
- **microscopic**: so small that we need a microscope to see it
- **naked eye**: our eyes without any tool or help
- **electron microscope**: a very strong microscope used today to magnify and observe cells
- **magnify**: to make something look bigger
- **tissue**: a group of cells that work together
- **Cell Biology**: the branch of science that studies cells

## Practice questions

1. What are all living things made of?
2. Why can we not see a cell with our naked eyes?
3. What is the name of the science that studies cells?
4. Who first used the word "cell", and in which year did he publish his book *Micrographia*?
5. You are a living thing. What does this tell you about the cells in your body?

## Answers

1. All living things are made of one or more cells.
2. Cells are very small and microscopic, so we need a microscope to see them.
3. The science that studies cells is called Cell Biology.
4. Hooke first used the word "cell", and he published *Micrographia* in 1665.
5. Since you are a living thing, your body is made of cells.

## Note for the teacher

This lesson is based only on the SCERT passage "The Cell"; the NCERT passages (soil and matter) do not cover cells, so they are used only to link to the magnifying lens and tiny organisms. The SCERT passage does not say what a cell is made of or describe its parts, so these were left out, and the Latin word is written as "cellua" in the source, which you may wish to check.
