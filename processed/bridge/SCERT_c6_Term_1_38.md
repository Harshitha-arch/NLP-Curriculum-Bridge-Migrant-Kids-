## What you already know

You wash your hands before you eat, and you brush your teeth every day. When you do not keep your body clean, you can fall sick more easily. Some sicknesses are caused by tiny living things that you cannot see.

## Lesson

Today we learn about **microbes**. A microbe is a tiny living thing. You cannot see a microbe with your naked eye. You need a **microscope**, which is a tool that makes very small things look big.

Microbes are not all the same. Most of them belong to four main groups:

- Bacteria
- Virus
- Protozoa
- Fungi

When we do not keep our body clean, microbes can cause sickness. Some common problems are diarrhoea, tooth decay, athlete's foot (also called Madurai's foot), and dandruff.

Some diseases spread through water or food. Cholera and typhoid are bacterial diseases. They spread through contaminated water. Typhoid can also spread through contaminated food. Tetanus is a bacterial disease that spreads when a wound is contaminated with bacteria.

Some diseases spread through the air. When a sick person sneezes or coughs, tiny droplets go into the air. Pneumonia and tuberculosis spread when you breathe in these droplets.

Now let us look at the **virus**. A virus is an infective agent. It has a nucleic acid inside a protein coat. A virus can only multiply inside the cells of other living things. It can infect plants, animals and microorganisms. A virus enters normal cells and uses the cell's machinery to make more viruses. It can kill cells, damage them or change them, and this makes you sick.

A **disease** is a definite process in the body that has a set of signs and symptoms. A **disorder** is a problem in the way a body part works.

Think about this: Is a virus a living thing or a non-living thing? Discuss it with your classmates.

## Key words

- **microbe**: a tiny living thing that you cannot see with your naked eye
- **microscope**: a tool that makes very small things look bigger
- **bacteria**: one of the four main groups of microbes
- **virus**: an infective agent that can only multiply inside the cells of other living things
- **protozoa**: one of the four main groups of microbes
- **fungi**: one of the four main groups of microbes
- **disease**: a definite process in the body with its own set of signs and symptoms
- **disorder**: a problem in the way a body part works
- **transmission**: the way a disease passes from one person to another
- **vaccination**: a medicine given to protect the body from certain diseases

## Practice questions

1. Name the four main groups of microbes.
2. Why do you need a microscope to see microbes?
3. How does cholera spread?
4. Why can a virus make you sick only when it is inside living cells?
5. A friend has a bad cough. Which disease in the lesson could spread to you through the air, and how?

## Answers

1. The four main groups of microbes are bacteria, virus, protozoa and fungi.
2. Microbes are too small to be seen with the naked eye, so a microscope is needed.
3. Cholera spreads through contaminated water.
4. A virus can multiply only inside the cells of other living things, and it uses those cells to make more viruses.
5. Pneumonia or tuberculosis could spread to you through airborne droplets from your friend's cough.
