## What you already know

Think about a busy street where horns keep blowing, or a house where the TV is very loud. It can be hard to talk, sleep or think in such places. This is the kind of problem this lesson is about.

## Lesson

Have you ever been in a place where loud horns and music never stop? It is hard to think, talk or sleep there. This problem is called **noise pollution**.

Noise pollution is unpleasant or loud sound that spoils our **environment**. Our environment is the air, water, land and living things around us. Noise is a kind of pollution, just like smoke or dirty water.

We all like a quiet and peaceful place. Unpleasant or loud sounds disturb us.

What causes noise pollution? Loud music, the sounds of motor vehicles, fireworks and machines all cause it.

Noise pollution affects our health. Continuous noise, which means noise that does not stop, disturbs our sleep. It also makes it hard to study. Noise has been linked to **stress**, which is a feeling of being worried or tense all the time. It has also been linked to health problems such as **high blood pressure** and **hearing loss**. Loud noise or loud music can damage our ears.

Noise pollution also disturbs animals. Birds have to **communicate** (talk) louder so they can hear each other in noisy areas. Under water, noise from ships can make whales lose their way. Whales use sounds to **navigate**, which means to find their way.

How do we reduce noise pollution? Here are five simple steps:

1. Turn off your electronics when you are not using them.
2. Lower the volume when you watch TV or listen to music.
3. Remind drivers not to use the horn too much.
4. Avoid fireworks.
5. Speak, do not shout.

Quiet is good for us, for animals and for the whole environment. Which of these five steps can you do today?

## Key words

- **Noise pollution**: loud or unpleasant sound that spoils our environment
- **Environment**: the air, water, land and living things around us
- **Pollution**: anything that makes the environment dirty or harmful
- **Stress**: a feeling of being worried or tense
- **High blood pressure**: a health problem where the blood pushes too hard in the body
- **Hearing loss**: when a person cannot hear as well as before
- **Communicate**: to share information, for example by talking
- **Navigate**: to find the way from one place to another
- **Volume**: how loud a sound is
- **Fireworks**: small explosives that make bright lights and loud bangs

## Practice questions

1. What is noise pollution?
2. Name two things that cause noise pollution.
3. Why does continuous noise make it hard to study?
4. Why do birds have to talk louder in noisy areas?
5. Which one of the five ways to reduce noise pollution can you follow at home today? Why?

## Answers

1. Noise pollution is loud or unpleasant sound that spoils our environment.
2. Loud music and the sounds of motor vehicles cause noise pollution. Fireworks and machines also cause it.
3. Continuous noise disturbs our sleep and does not allow us to study.
4. Birds talk louder so that they can hear each other in noisy areas.
5. Any sensible answer that matches the lesson, for example "Turn off my electronics when I am not using them, because it saves me from loud noise." Accept any one of the five steps with a reason.
