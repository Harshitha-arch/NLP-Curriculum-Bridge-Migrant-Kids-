## What you already know

You wash your hands and clothes with soap every day. Some dirt and oil on clothes do not come off easily, so we need a special cleaner to remove them.

## Lesson

Soaps and detergents are cleaners that we use every day. Bathing soap and washing detergent are two kinds of soap that we use in our daily life.

A **molecule** is a very tiny particle. A detergent molecule has two sides. One side is **water loving**. It joins with water. The other side is **water hating**. It does not join with water.

Let us see how a detergent cleans a cloth. When we use washing powder to remove strong stains, the water hating side goes and joins with the dirt and oil in the cloth. The water loving side joins with the water molecules.

Next, we **agitate** the cloth. This means we move it and rub it. Now the dirt is surrounded by many detergent molecules, and it is taken away from the cloth. The cloth becomes clean.

The dirt surrounded by detergent molecules floats in the water. This is why the water becomes dirty after washing.

Soaps are not all the same. Different soaps are made for different purposes, using different **raw materials**. Raw materials are the basic materials we start with to make something. The nature of a soap depends on its **ingredients**, which are the things it is made from.

In Activity 3, you collect soap wrappers. You read the ingredients on each wrapper. You then fill in a table for bathing soap, washing soap, bathing soap for kids, toilet cleaners, and house floor cleaner liquid.

You can also make soap yourself. Put newspaper on your work area. Take 35 ml of water in a jar. Add 10 g of **sodium hydroxide**, also called **lye**, and let it cool. Then add 60 ml of coconut oil, drop by drop, and stir well. Pour the mixture into an empty match box. When it dries, you get soap. You can try this soap to wash your handkerchief.

## Key words

- **Soap**: a cleaner that removes dirt and oil from skin and cloth.
- **Detergent**: a cleaning substance, such as washing powder, that removes strong stains.
- **Molecule**: a very tiny particle of a substance.
- **Water loving**: the side of a detergent molecule that joins with water.
- **Water hating**: the side of a detergent molecule that joins with dirt and oil.
- **Agitate**: to move or rub something to help clean it.
- **Ingredients**: the things that a soap or product is made from.
- **Sodium hydroxide (lye)**: the chemical used with coconut oil to make soap in the activity.

## Practice questions

1. Name two kinds of soap that we use in our daily life.
2. What are the two sides of a detergent molecule?
3. Which side of a detergent molecule joins with dirt and oil in the cloth?
4. Why does the water become dirty after we wash a cloth?
5. Why are different soaps, such as bathing soap and toilet cleaner, not all the same?

## Answers

1. Bathing soap and washing detergent are two kinds of soap that we use every day.
2. A detergent molecule has one water loving side and one water hating side.
3. The water hating side joins with the dirt and oil in the cloth.
4. The dirt, surrounded by detergent molecules, floats in the water, so the water becomes dirty.
5. The nature of a soap depends on its ingredients, and different soaps are made with different raw materials.
