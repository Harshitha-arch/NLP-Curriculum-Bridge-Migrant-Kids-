## What you already know

You have learned from NCERT that most water on Earth is salty, and that fresh water is found in ice sheets, rivers, lakes and underground. This lesson sorts those fresh water sources into three kinds.

## Lesson

You know that most of Earth's water is salty. The water we can drink and use for farming comes from fresh water sources. Scientists group these sources into three kinds.

**1. Surface water**
Surface water is water on the top of the Earth. You can see it. Rivers, lakes, ponds and streams are surface water. So are freshwater wetlands. A wetland is a place where land is covered with water for part of the year.

**2. Frozen water**
Frozen water is water that has turned into ice. It is found in polar ice-caps and glaciers. A glacier is a huge slow-moving mass of ice. An ice-cap is a thick layer of ice that covers land.

Frozen water is the largest part of our fresh water. About 68.7% of all the fresh water available on Earth is in frozen form.

The Himalayas have ice caps, icebergs and glaciers. Ten of Asia's largest rivers flow from the Himalayas. More than a billion people depend on these rivers for their livelihood, which means the way they earn a living and meet their needs.

Frozen water also protects life in cold places. In winter, a layer of ice forms on top of a lake or pond. The ice acts like a warm coat. It stops heat from escaping from the water below. So the water under the ice stays liquid, and the fish and other water animals living there do not die.

**3. Ground water**
Ground water is water that is present under the Earth's surface, in the soil. We get ground water through springs, open wells, tube wells and hand pumps.

**An example from the passage**
Estuaries are wetlands where rivers and other water bodies meet the sea. Here, fresh water from the land mixes with salty sea water. Estuaries are home to special plants and animals.

So, the three natural sources of fresh water are surface water, frozen water and ground water.

## Key words

- **Surface water**: water that is on top of the land, such as in rivers, lakes and ponds
- **Frozen water**: water that has turned into ice, such as in glaciers and ice-caps
- **Ground water**: water that is under the Earth's surface, in soil
- **Glacier**: a very large, slow-moving mass of ice
- **Ice-cap**: a thick layer of ice that covers land
- **Wetland**: land that is covered with water for part of the year
- **Estuary**: a wetland where a river meets the sea
- **Spring**: a place where ground water comes out naturally at the surface
- **Tube well**: a deep well from which ground water is pumped up
- **Livelihood**: the way a person earns money and meets basic needs

## Practice questions

1. Name the three natural sources of fresh water.
2. Why do fish and other water animals survive under a frozen lake in winter?
3. Where do ten of Asia's largest rivers begin?
4. Name two ways people get ground water.
5. Which natural source of fresh water is near your home? Is it surface water, frozen water or ground water?

## Answers

1. The three natural sources of fresh water are surface water, frozen water and ground water.
2. A layer of ice on top acts like a coat and stops heat from escaping, so the water below stays liquid and the animals do not die.
3. Ten of Asia's largest rivers flow from the Himalayas.
4. People get ground water through springs, open wells, tube wells or hand pumps.
5. Answers will vary. For example, a river or pond is surface water, and water from a hand pump is ground water.
