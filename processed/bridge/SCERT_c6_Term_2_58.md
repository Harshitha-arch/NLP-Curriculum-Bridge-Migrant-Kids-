## What you already know
You use muscles every time you wave, pick up your school bag, or write with your hand. Your arm moves because muscles pull on it. Today you will learn how this works in the whole body.

## Lesson
Your body moves because of your **muscles**. The **muscular system** is the group of muscles in your body. It works with the bones (skeletal system) and the nerves (nervous system) to move the body. Muscles also help keep your **posture**, which is how your body is held and positioned.

Muscles can **contract**. To contract means to become short and thick. When a muscle contracts, it helps move the part of the body it is joined to.

Muscles can only **pull**. They cannot push. So two muscles work together to move a bone at a joint, such as the elbow. When one muscle contracts, the other muscle **relaxes**. To relax means to become long and thin again.

Let us see this with your arm. The **biceps** is the muscle at the front of the upper arm. The **triceps** is the muscle at the back.

- When you raise your lower arm, the biceps contracts and becomes short. The triceps relaxes and stretches, and it helps pull the arm up.
- When you lower your arm, the triceps contracts and the biceps relaxes.

Try it. Move your lower arm up and down gently. Feel the front and back muscles go tight and soft.

There are three types of muscles:

1. **Skeletal muscles** are attached to bones. They are **voluntary**, which means we can control them with our will (our own choice). The muscles of the arm are an example.
2. **Smooth muscles** are found in the walls of the digestive tract (the body parts that deal with food), the urinary bladder, arteries, and other internal organs. They are **involuntary**, which means we cannot control them with our will.
3. **Cardiac muscles** make up the walls of the heart. They contract continuously and rhythmically, and they are involuntary.

## Key words
- **muscle**: a part of the body that contracts and helps move other parts
- **contract**: to become short and thick
- **relax**: to become long and thin again
- **skeletal muscle**: a muscle attached to bones
- **smooth muscle**: a muscle found in internal organs such as the stomach and bladder
- **cardiac muscle**: the muscle that makes up the walls of the heart
- **biceps**: the muscle at the front of the upper arm that bends the arm at the elbow
- **triceps**: the muscle at the back of the upper arm that straightens the elbow
- **voluntary**: controlled by our will
- **involuntary**: not controlled by our will

## Practice questions
1. What does a muscle do when it contracts?
2. What is the name of the muscle at the front of the upper arm?
3. Why do we need two muscles to move a bone at a joint?
4. Which type of muscle makes up the walls of the heart, and is it voluntary or involuntary?
5. When you lower your arm slowly, which muscle contracts?

## Answers
1. When a muscle contracts, it becomes short and thick and helps move the part of the body it is joined to.
2. The muscle at the front of the upper arm is the biceps.
3. We need two muscles because muscles can only pull, not push, so one pulls and the other relaxes to move the bone back.
4. Cardiac muscle makes up the walls of the heart, and it is involuntary.
5. When you lower your arm, the triceps contracts.
