## What you already know

You know that the Sun gives us light and warmth, and that it is the main source of energy on Earth. Plants use sunlight to make food. In this lesson you will learn more about where heat comes from.

## Lesson

We all know the Sun gives us light. Does it also give us heat?

Try this. Stand in the sunlight for some time. Then touch your head. It feels hot. That is because the Sun gives out heat as well as light.

This is why it can be hard to walk barefoot on a sunny afternoon. Now you can understand why.

The Sun is one source of heat. Burning is another. When we burn a fuel, we get heat energy. A fuel is a material that we burn to get heat. Wood, kerosene, coal, charcoal, petrol and oil are all fuels.

Think about your home. How do you get heat to cook food? Discuss this with your teacher and classmates.

So what is heat? Everything is made of very tiny parts called particles. Heat is the total kinetic energy of these particles. Kinetic energy is the energy of moving things. So heat is the energy of all the tiny particles in an object.

Heat is measured in joules. The joule is the SI unit of heat. The unit calorie is also used.

## Key words

- **heat**: a form of energy that makes things feel hot
- **energy**: the ability to do work or cause change
- **source**: the place from which something comes
- **combustion**: burning, which gives out heat
- **fuel**: a material that we burn to get heat
- **particles**: very tiny parts that make up everything
- **kinetic energy**: the energy of moving things
- **joule**: the SI unit used to measure heat
- **calorie**: another unit used to measure heat

## Practice questions

1. What does the Sun give out besides light?
2. Name two fuels that can be burned to get heat.
3. What is the SI unit of heat?
4. Why does your head feel hot after you stand in the sunlight?
5. Which of these can be burned to make heat: wood, charcoal, or sand?

## Answers

1. The Sun gives out heat besides light.
2. Wood and coal are two fuels that can be burned to get heat. (Kerosene, charcoal, petrol and oil also work.)
3. The SI unit of heat is the joule.
4. Your head feels hot because the Sun gives out heat as well as light.
5. Wood and charcoal can be burned to make heat.
