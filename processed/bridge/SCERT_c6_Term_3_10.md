## What you already know
You have seen that two like poles of magnets push each other away, and two unlike poles pull each other together. Today you will see how this push and pull can lift and move a train.

## Lesson
Today we learn about a special train called a **Maglev train**. Maglev stands for **Magnetic Levitation**.

**Levitation** means lifting up and floating in the air. In a Maglev train, the train is lifted by **repulsion**. Repulsion means pushing away. Like poles of magnets repel each other. So the magnets in the train push against magnets on the track, and the train floats above the track.

The train also moves forward. This is called **propulsion**, which means pushing or driving forward. The train uses **attraction** and **repulsion** at the same time. Attraction means pulling together. Unlike poles attract each other. The magnets are controlled by **electricity**, so the train can be made to move.

Maglev trains have some key features. They have no **friction**. Friction is a force that rubs against movement and slows things down. Maglev trains also have high speed and make no noise. A Maglev train has a driver cabin and passenger seats, like other trains.

Three countries are using Maglev trains for public transport now. They are China, Japan and South Korea. Many other countries are looking at using them too.

In India, routes for Maglev trains have been considered for proposal. These are Mumbai–Delhi, Mumbai–Nagpur, and Chennai–Bengaluru–Mysuru.

Think about it this way. A normal train runs on wheels and rails. A Maglev train floats on magnetic push and pull, so it does not touch the track.

## Key words
- **Maglev**: short name for magnetic levitation, a train that floats on magnets.
- **Levitation**: lifting up and floating in the air.
- **Repulsion**: pushing away. Like poles of magnets repel.
- **Attraction**: pulling towards each other. Unlike poles attract.
- **Propulsion**: pushing or driving something forward.
- **Friction**: a rubbing force that slows down movement.
- **Electricity**: a form of energy that can control the magnets in a Maglev train.
- **Poles**: the two ends of a magnet.

## Practice questions
1. What does Maglev stand for?
2. What does "levitation" mean?
3. How does a Maglev train move forward?
4. Name two key features of Maglev trains from the lesson.
5. Which of the proposed Indian routes includes Mumbai?

## Answers
1. Maglev stands for Magnetic Levitation.
2. Levitation means lifting up and floating in the air.
3. A Maglev train moves forward by using attraction and repulsion at the same time, with magnets controlled by electricity.
4. Maglev trains have no friction, high speed, and no noise. (Any two.)
5. Mumbai–Delhi and Mumbai–Nagpur include Mumbai.
