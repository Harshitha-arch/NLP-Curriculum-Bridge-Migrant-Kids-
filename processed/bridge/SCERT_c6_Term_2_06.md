## What you already know
From NCERT, you learned that a clinical thermometer measures body temperature in degree Celsius (°C). You also learned that your sense of touch is not always reliable for judging whether something is hot or cold. Today you will learn that heat and temperature are two different things.

## Lesson
Many people use the words *heat* and *temperature* as if they mean the same thing. They do not. Each word has its own meaning.

Everything is made of tiny parts called **molecules**. Molecules are always moving or shaking. We cannot see them.

**Temperature** tells us how fast the molecules move or vibrate (shake). Temperature measures the **average** energy of the molecules. Average means the typical amount for each molecule.

**Heat** is different. Heat measures the **total** energy of all the molecules in a substance. Total means all of them added together. So heat depends on two things: the temperature, and how many molecules there are.

Let us see this with water.

Put 1 litre of water in one pan and 5 litres of water in another pan. Heat both on the same stove. The 1 litre water starts to boil first. The 5 litre water takes longer. Both pans show 100 °C when the water boils. Both have the same temperature, but the 5 litre pan has more molecules. It needs more heat to boil. So the 5 litre boiling water has more heat energy.

Now place an open can of lukewarm water in each pan. The can in the 5 litre pan gets hotter. The 5 litre water gives more heat energy to the water in the can, even though both pans are at 100 °C.

Here is another example. Pavithra has a cup of hot tea. The tea is hotter than the water in the pond near her house. But the pond has a very large amount of water. It has far more molecules than the cup of tea. So the pond has more heat energy than the cup of tea.

Remember: **temperature** tells how hot something is. **Heat** tells how much heat energy is in the whole substance.

We measure temperature in degrees Celsius (°C). Our normal body temperature is 37 °C. Water freezes at 0 °C. When the temperature is below 0 °C, we write a minus sign before the number.

## Key words
- **Temperature**: how hot or cold something is, based on how fast its molecules move
- **Heat**: the total energy of all the molecules in a substance
- **Molecule**: a tiny part of a substance that is always moving or shaking
- **Kinetic energy**: the energy that a moving thing has
- **Average**: the typical value for each one, found by sharing the total out equally
- **Heat energy**: the energy that heat carries from a hotter thing to a colder thing
- **Boiling point**: the temperature at which water starts to boil (100 °C)
- **Freezing point**: the temperature at which water becomes ice (0 °C)

## Practice questions
1. What does temperature tell us about the molecules in a substance?
2. What does heat measure?
3. In the stove activity, which pan of water started to boil first: the 1 litre pan or the 5 litre pan?
4. Both pans show 100 °C when the water boils. Why does the 5 litre pan have more heat energy?
5. Pavithra's tea is hotter than the pond water. Which has more heat energy, the cup of tea or the pond? Why?

## Answers
1. Temperature tells us how fast the molecules move or vibrate.
2. Heat measures the total energy of all the molecules in a substance.
3. The 1 litre pan of water started to boil first.
4. The 5 litre pan has more molecules, so it has more heat energy even though both are at 100 °C.
5. The pond has more heat energy, because it has a very large amount of water and so more molecules, even though the tea is hotter.
