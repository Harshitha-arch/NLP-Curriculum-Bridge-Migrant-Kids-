## What you already know
You know that moving air is called wind, and that we need oxygen from the air to live. Today you will see that air is also inside a bottle that looks empty, and that air high above the earth protects us.

## Lesson
Air is everywhere. We cannot see it, but we can feel it. When trees rustle, clothes sway on a line, or kites fly, air is moving.

Is a glass bottle really empty? Take an empty glass bottle and turn it upside down. Something is still inside it. Let us find out what.

Dip the open mouth of the bottle into a trough of water. Water does not enter the bottle. The air inside has no way out, so the water cannot go in.

Now tilt the bottle slightly and dip it again. Bubbles come out of the bottle. Water fills the space that the air was taking. The air was inside the bottle all along. So air fills all the space inside the bottle.

Air is needed for life. We can live without food for some days and without water for a few hours. But we cannot live without air for more than a few minutes. Air is also needed for breathing and for combustion, which means burning.

Moving air has different names. Gentle, cool air is a breeze. Air moving with great force is a storm. A storm can uproot trees and blow off roof tops.

The air around the earth is called the atmosphere. The atmosphere has layers. The stratosphere lies above the troposphere. The stratosphere has the ozone layer in it. The ozone layer protects all life on earth from harmful ultraviolet rays of the sun. So air protects us, even high up in the sky.

## Key words
- **atmosphere**: the air that surrounds the earth
- **combustion**: burning
- **breeze**: gentle, cool moving air
- **storm**: air moving with great force
- **troposphere**: a layer of the atmosphere, below the stratosphere
- **stratosphere**: a layer of the atmosphere, above the troposphere
- **ozone layer**: a layer in the stratosphere that protects life on earth
- **ultraviolet rays**: harmful rays that come from the sun

## Practice questions
1. What was inside the empty-looking glass bottle?
2. Why does water not enter the bottle when it is pushed in upside down?
3. What happens to the water when the bottle is tilted and dipped again?
4. What does the ozone layer protect us from?
5. A strong wind blew off the roof of a house. What is this kind of moving air called?

## Answers
1. Air was inside the bottle.
2. There is no space for the air to escape, so the water cannot enter.
3. Bubbles of air come out, and water fills the space the air had taken.
4. It protects all life on earth from harmful ultraviolet rays of the sun.
5. It is called a storm, because air moving with great force is a storm.

## Note for the teacher
This lesson is based on the SCERT "Air" passage, with the NCERT passage used only for the "What you already know" section. The SCERT passage has headings but no text on composition, the experiments for oxygen, nitrogen and carbon dioxide, combustion, or plant survival, so these are not taught here. The sentence about aircraft ("fly above this layer") is incomplete in the source, so I wrote it as the stratosphere and troposphere facts only; please check it against the full textbook.
