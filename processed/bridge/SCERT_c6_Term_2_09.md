## What you already know

You know that a solid, such as a stone, a wooden stick or an iron nail, keeps its own shape. Think about how a metal object feels when it is hot or cold in the sun. This lesson asks what heat does to the size of a solid.

## Lesson

A solid has a **definite shape**. This means it keeps its shape until something changes it.

When a solid is **heated**, it gets bigger. It **expands**, which means it grows in size. It grows in all directions. Its length increases, its area increases, and its volume increases.

The growth in **length** is called **linear expansion**. "Linear" means along a line, so this is growth along one direction.

The growth in **volume** is called **cubical expansion**. Volume is the space an object takes up.

The passage asks two questions. Why is the iron rim of a bullock cart wheel heated before it is put on the wheel? Why is a small gap left between two lengths of railway lines? The passage says an experiment can help us find the answers.

**The cycle spoke experiment**

In the experiment, a cycle spoke is fixed so that its free end touches a coin. The coin is part of an electric **circuit**. A circuit is a closed path that electricity can travel along. A **dry cell** and a **bulb** are connected to the circuit. When the spoke touches the coin, the circuit is complete and the bulb lights up.

First, slide a sheet of paper between the spoke and the coin, then take it out. Now there is a small gap between them. The bulb does not light up, because the spoke does not touch the coin.

Next, heat the spoke with a candle. After some time, the spoke touches the coin, and the bulb lights up. This happens because the spoke gets longer when it is heated, so its end reaches the coin. When the candle is taken away, the spoke cools, and the bulb goes off some time later.

So, the length of the spoke increases when it is heated.

**Activity: Cubical expansion**

Take a metal ring and a metal ball. Choose a ball that just passes through the ring. Then heat the ball and check whether it still passes through the ring.

## Key words

- **Solid**: a material that keeps its own shape.
- **Definite shape**: a fixed shape that does not change on its own.
- **Expand**: to grow bigger in size.
- **Linear expansion**: growth in length, along a line.
- **Cubical expansion**: growth in volume, the space an object takes up.
- **Volume**: the amount of space an object takes up.
- **Circuit**: a closed path along which electricity can flow.
- **Dry cell**: a battery that gives electricity to a circuit.
- **Bulb**: a light that glows when electricity flows through it.

## Practice questions

1. What happens to a solid when it is heated?
2. What is the name of the growth in length of a solid?
3. In the cycle spoke experiment, why did the bulb light up after the spoke was heated?
4. Why did the bulb go off after the candle was taken away?
5. A metal lid of a jar is tight. Why might warming it help you open the jar? Explain using the idea of expansion.

## Answers

1. When a solid is heated, it expands, so its length, area and volume all increase.
2. The growth in length is called linear expansion.
3. The spoke got longer when it was heated, so its end touched the coin and completed the circuit.
4. The spoke cooled down, so it became shorter and its end no longer touched the coin.
5. Warming the lid makes it expand, so it can grow a little bigger and loosen its fit on the jar.

## Note for the teacher

This lesson is based on the SCERT passage on linear and cubical expansion, with the cycle spoke experiment and the ball and ring activity. The passage asks why the iron rim is heated and why a gap is left between railway lines, but it does not state the answers, so the lesson does not give them; question 5 uses the lid example as an extension of the idea. The passage also does not state the result of the ball and ring activity, so the teacher should let children observe it.
