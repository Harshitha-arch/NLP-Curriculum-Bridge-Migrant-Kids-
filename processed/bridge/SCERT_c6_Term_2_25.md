## What you already know
In your NCERT book, you read about a bean seed that grows into a plant, makes flowers and pods, and then makes new seeds. That is a change too. Changes happen all around us, both quickly and slowly.

## Lesson
Change is the law of nature. A change is when something becomes different from what it was before. We can see the difference between the start and the end.

Changes can be sorted into groups. Each group tells us something different about a change.

**1. Slow and fast changes.** Some changes happen very quickly. Others take hours, days or even years. Paper burns quickly. An iron nail takes a few days to rust. Milk takes a few hours to turn into curd. Vegetables get soft in a few minutes when we cook them.

**2. Temporary and permanent changes.** A temporary change does not last. A permanent change stays. Weather and seasons change again and again in a pattern. We say they change periodically.

**3. Reversible and irreversible changes.** An irreversible change cannot be undone. We cannot get back the original state. A reversible change can be undone.

**4. Physical and chemical changes.** Some changes produce new substances. Others do not. A substance is a kind of material. When a new substance is made, the change is different from one that makes no new substance.

**5. Natural and man-made changes.** Some changes happen by themselves in nature. Others are made by human beings.

**6. Desirable and undesirable changes.** A desirable change is one we want. An undesirable change is one we do not want. The same change can be good for one person and bad for another.

Every change shows a difference in one or more properties. These are shape, colour, temperature, position or composition. Some changes are easy to see. Others are hard to notice.

## Key words
- **change**: when something becomes different from what it was before
- **temporary**: lasts only for a short time
- **permanent**: stays for a long time
- **reversible**: can be undone
- **irreversible**: cannot be undone, so we cannot get back the original state
- **substance**: a kind of material
- **natural**: happens by itself in nature
- **desirable**: something we want
- **undesirable**: something we do not want
- **periodically**: again and again in a pattern

## Practice questions
1. What is a change?
2. How long does it take for an iron nail to rust?
3. What is an irreversible change?
4. What is the difference between a change that produces a new substance and a change that does not?
5. Think of one change you saw today. Is it natural or made by human beings?

## Answers
1. A change is when something becomes different from what it was before.
2. An iron nail takes a few days to rust.
3. An irreversible change is a change that cannot be undone, so we cannot get back the original state.
4. A change that produces a new substance makes a new kind of material. A change that does not produce a new substance does not make one.
5. Answers will vary. A child may say a natural change, such as a plant growing, or a change made by people, such as cooking food.

## Note for the teacher
This lesson is based on the SCERT passage "Classification of Changes", with the NCERT bean-seed passage used only for the opening link. The SCERT passage does not define physical or chemical change, does not define reversible change, and gives no example of a reversible change, so these parts are explained in simple terms or linked to the passage's "new substances" statement. The picture-based questions, the balloon activity answers and the garbled sections (such as the "Touch me not plant" line) were left out.
