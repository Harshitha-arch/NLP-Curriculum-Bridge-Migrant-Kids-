## What you already know

When you walk, run, chew your food, or dance, your body moves. Your bones and muscles work together to make these movements possible.

## Lesson

Today we learn about the **skeletal system**. This is the group of parts that gives your body its shape and helps it move.

The skeletal system has three main parts: **bones**, **cartilages**, and **joints**. Bones give your body a frame, like the frame of a house. Bones work with muscles to help you move. When you walk, run, chew, or dance, your bones and muscles work together.

An adult has **206 bones**. A baby is born with more than 300 bones. As the baby grows, some bones join together. This is why an adult has fewer bones.

Bones are joined to other parts of the body in different ways. A **ligament** connects one bone to another bone. A **tendon** connects a bone to a muscle.

Scientists divide the skeleton into two main parts:

- The **axial skeleton** runs up the middle of the body. It includes the **skull**, the **vertebral column** (the spine), and the **rib cage**.
- The **appendicular skeleton** includes the bones of the arms and legs. These are the **limbs**. The arms are the upper limbs, and the legs are the lower limbs.

The skull protects your brain and the bones of your face. The lower jaw is the largest and strongest bone in your face.

The vertebral column is made of many small bones called **vertebrae**. It starts at the base of the skull. It protects the **spinal cord**.

The rib cage has 12 pairs of curved, flat bones. It protects the heart and lungs.

Now try an activity. Ask a friend to tie a wooden scale to your arm so that your elbow is in the middle. Try to bend your elbow. You cannot. Why? A single bone cannot bend. Your elbow bends because two bones meet there and move at that joint.

So, we can bend or move our body only at the points where bones meet.

## Key words

- **Skeletal system**: the bones, cartilages and joints that support and shape the body.
- **Bone**: a hard part of the body that forms the frame of the body.
- **Cartilage**: a firm, flexible body tissue found along with bones.
- **Joint**: the place where two bones meet.
- **Ligament**: a strong band that connects one bone to another bone.
- **Tendon**: a strong band that connects a bone to a muscle.
- **Skull**: the bones that protect the brain and form the face.
- **Vertebra**: one of the small bones that make up the spine. (Plural: vertebrae.)
- **Rib cage**: the curved bones around the chest that protect the heart and lungs.
- **Limbs**: the arms and legs of the body.

## Practice questions

1. What are the three main parts of the skeletal system?
2. What is the difference between a ligament and a tendon?
3. Name the two major divisions of the skeletal system.
4. Why can you not bend your elbow when a wooden scale is tied to your arm?
5. Chewing and dancing need bones and muscles to work together. Which bone protects your brain, and why is it important when you chew?

## Answers

1. The three main parts are bones, cartilages, and joints.
2. A ligament connects bone to bone, while a tendon connects bone to muscle.
3. The two major divisions are the axial skeleton and the appendicular skeleton.
4. A single bone cannot bend, and the elbow bends only where two bones meet.
5. The skull protects the brain. Chewing uses the jaw, and the skull is part of the frame that holds the face and head together.
