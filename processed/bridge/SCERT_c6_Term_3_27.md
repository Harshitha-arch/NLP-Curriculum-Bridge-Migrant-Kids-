## What you already know
You know that a plant needs water and sunlight to grow. This lesson tells you that plants also need nutrients, which they take from the soil.

## Lesson
Plants need more than water, sunlight and air to grow. They also need **nutrients**. A nutrient is a useful material that a plant takes in to grow.

Plants get their nutrients from the soil. Three nutrients are very important. They are **nitrogen** (N), **phosphorus** (P) and **potassium** (K). These three are called **principal nutrients**. "Principal" means main or most important.

Think about a field where crops are grown again and again. Each crop takes nutrients out of the soil. So the soil can lose nutrients over time. How can we put these nutrients back? We add **fertilizers**. A fertilizer is a material that we add to the soil to give one or more nutrients to the soil.

There are two kinds of fertilizers.

**Organic fertilizers** are made from plant or animal materials. Some are made by **microorganisms**, which are tiny living things too small to see. Organic fertilizers are easy to prepare, and they are cheap. Compost and vermicompost are examples.

**Inorganic fertilizers** are made from natural elements. These elements go through **chemical changes** in factories. A chemical change makes a new material. Urea, ammonium sulphate, super phosphate and potassium nitrate are examples.

Here is an example with urea. Urea contains 46 percent nitrogen. This means that if we add 50 kg of urea to the soil, 23 kg of nitrogen goes into the soil. Check: 46 percent of 50 kg is 23 kg.

Remember: plants need nutrients from the soil. When the soil loses nutrients, we add fertilizers to give them back.

## Key words
- **nutrient**: a useful material that plants need to grow
- **fertilizer**: a material we add to soil to give it nutrients
- **organic fertilizer**: a fertilizer made from plant or animal materials, or made by microorganisms
- **inorganic fertilizer**: a fertilizer made from natural elements that go through chemical changes in factories
- **principal nutrients**: the three main nutrients plants need: nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium
- **nitrogen**: one of the three principal nutrients (N)
- **phosphorus**: one of the three principal nutrients (P)
- **potassium**: one of the three principal nutrients (K)
- **microorganism**: a tiny living thing that is too small to see
- **chemical change**: a change that makes a new material

## Practice questions
1. What are the three principal nutrients?
2. Where do plants get their nutrients?
3. What is the difference between organic and inorganic fertilizers? Give one example of each.
4. If we add 50 kg of urea to the soil, how much nitrogen goes into the soil? Why?
5. A farmer grows crops in the same field again and again. Why might the farmer need to add fertilizers?

## Answers
1. The three principal nutrients are nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium.
2. Plants get their nutrients from the soil.
3. Organic fertilizers are made from plant or animal materials, such as compost. Inorganic fertilizers are made from natural elements through chemical changes in factories, such as urea.
4. 23 kg of nitrogen goes into the soil, because urea contains 46 percent nitrogen and 46 percent of 50 kg is 23 kg.
5. Crops take nutrients from the soil, so the soil can lose nutrients. Fertilizers put nutrients back into the soil.
