## What you already know
You have probably cut your finger and seen red blood. When you run hard, your heart beats fast, and you can feel it in your chest.

## Lesson
Your body has a **circulatory system**. It is a group of parts that work together to move things around your body. The main parts are the **heart**, the **blood vessels**, and the **blood**.

**The heart** is in your chest, between your two lungs. It is shaped like a pump and has four chambers, or rooms. A thin two-layered covering called the pericardium surrounds it. The heart pumps blood all the time, for your whole life.

**Blood vessels** are the tubes that blood moves through. There are three kinds: arteries, veins, and capillaries. Together they make a closed network, so the blood moves around in a loop.

**Blood** is a red fluid. It has two main parts: plasma (the liquid part) and blood cells. There are three kinds of blood cells:
- Red blood cells (RBCs) carry gases. They are made in the bone marrow, the soft tissue inside some of your bones.
- White blood cells (WBCs) help protect the body.
- Platelets are small pieces that help blood in other ways.

**What does the circulatory system do?** It carries breathing gases, food materials called nutrients, hormones, and waste materials around the body. It protects the body from harmful pathogens, which are germs that can cause illness. It also helps control your body temperature.

**Let's test the air we breathe out.** Take two jars of lime water and a straw. Leave the first jar alone. Blow air into the second jar through the straw. In a few seconds, the lime water in the second jar turns milky. This shows that the air we breathe out has carbon dioxide (CO2) in it.

**Feel your pulse.** Place your middle and index fingers on the inside of your left wrist. You will feel a throbbing movement. This is the **pulse**. It happens because blood is flowing in the arteries. The number of beats in one minute is the **pulse rate**. A resting person usually has a pulse rate between 72 and 80 beats per minute. You can also feel your pulse in other places on your body.

## Key words
- **circulatory system**: the body parts that move blood and other materials around the body
- **heart**: the body part that pumps blood
- **blood vessels**: tubes that blood flows through
- **blood**: the red fluid in the body that carries materials
- **plasma**: the liquid part of blood
- **red blood cells (RBCs)**: blood cells that are made in the bone marrow
- **white blood cells (WBCs)**: blood cells that help protect the body
- **platelets**: small blood parts that help blood in other ways
- **pathogens**: germs that can harm the body
- **pulse rate**: the number of pulse beats in one minute

## Practice questions
1. Name the three main parts of the circulatory system.
2. Why does the lime water in the second jar turn milky?
3. What is the pulse rate, and what is the usual pulse rate of a resting person?
4. Put two fingers on your left wrist and count your pulse for one minute. Compare your number with a classmate's.

## Answers
1. The three main parts are the heart, blood vessels, and blood.
2. The lime water turns milky because the air we breathe out contains carbon dioxide.
3. The pulse rate is the number of pulse beats in one minute. A resting person usually has a pulse rate between 72 and 80 beats per minute.
4. Your count may be different from your classmate's. Compare the two numbers to see how pulse rates can vary.
