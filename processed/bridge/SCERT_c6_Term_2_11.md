## What you already know

You have seen water droplets form on the outside of a cold glass with ice in it. Today we look at another change caused by heat: how solid things change size when they get hot.

## Lesson

Heat can change the size of solid things.

When a solid gets hot, it **expands**. This means it grows bigger. When the heat is taken away, the solid comes back to its original size.

**Railway tracks.** Gaps are left between the rails of a railway track. Rails expand when they get hot. If there were no gaps, the rails would have no room to grow and could push against each other. The gaps give them space to expand.

**Concrete bridges.** Gaps are also left between the joints of a concrete bridge. The bridge needs room to change length when it gets hot.

**Electric wires.** Wires between electric posts contract on cold days. This means they become shorter. In summer they sag, which means they droop lower. To solve this, we leave the wires **slack**, which means loose, so they are free to change length.

**A thick glass tumbler.** Glass is a poor conductor of heat. A poor conductor does not pass heat through itself easily. Suppose hot liquid is poured into a thick glass tumbler. The inside surface gets hot and expands. The outside surface stays at room temperature and does not expand. This is called **unequal expansion**. The inside and outside grow by different amounts, so the glass cracks.

**Kitchen and lab glassware.** Glassware used in kitchens and laboratories is often made of **borosilicate glass**, also called pyrex glass. Borosilicate glass does not expand much when heated. So it does not crack.

## Key words

- **expand**: to grow bigger in size
- **contract**: to become smaller in size
- **solid**: a thing that keeps its own shape, such as a rail or a wire
- **poor conductor**: a material that does not let heat pass through it easily
- **unequal expansion**: when one part of a thing grows more than another part
- **slack**: loose, not pulled tight
- **sag**: to droop or hang lower
- **borosilicate glass**: a special glass that does not expand much when heated

## Practice questions

1. What happens to a solid when it gets hot?
2. Why are gaps left between the rails of a railway track?
3. Why are electric wires left slack between electric posts?
4. Why does a thick glass tumbler crack when hot liquid is poured into it?
5. Why is borosilicate glass used for kitchen and laboratory glassware?

## Answers

1. When a solid gets hot, it expands, which means it grows bigger.
2. Rails expand when they get hot, so the gaps give them room to grow without pushing against each other.
3. Wires contract on cold days and sag in summer, so leaving them slack lets them change length safely.
4. The inside of the glass gets hot and expands, but the outside does not, so the glass cracks.
5. Borosilicate glass does not expand much when heated, so it does not crack.
