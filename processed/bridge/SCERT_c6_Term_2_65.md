## What you already know

You learned in NCERT that living things need water, and you use water every day for drinking, cooking and bathing. Your body also needs water, and this lesson explains where the body gets rid of its waste.

## Lesson

Your body is made of many parts that work together. Some of these parts are called a **system**. Today we learn about the **excretory system**. This system removes waste from your body.

Your body makes waste while it works. One kind of waste is **nitrogenous waste**. This is waste that contains nitrogen. The excretory system removes these wastes from the body.

The excretory system has four main parts: the **kidneys**, the **ureters**, the **urinary bladder** and the **urethra**.

The kidneys are the most important part. They are bean-shaped and sit inside your abdomen, which is the part of the body below your chest. Each kidney is made of many tiny units called **nephrons**. Nephrons **filter** the blood. To filter means to take out the unwanted parts. The nephrons take the waste out of the blood and make **urine**. Urine is the liquid that carries waste out of the body.

Water is part of this process. About 70 percent of your body is water. You need to drink water every day. The passage says a person normally takes in about 1.5 to 3.5 litres of water each day, in food and in drinks.

Water also leaves the body. It goes out in two ways: as **sweat** and as urine. So the water we drink helps the body keep working and also helps it remove waste.

Remember these points:
- The excretory system removes nitrogenous wastes from the body.
- The kidneys filter the blood and form urine.
- Water leaves the body as sweat and urine.
- We need to drink water every day.

## Key words

- **Excretory system**: the parts of the body that remove waste.
- **Nitrogenous waste**: waste material that contains nitrogen.
- **Kidney**: a bean-shaped organ that filters blood and makes urine.
- **Ureter**: a tube that carries urine from the kidney.
- **Urinary bladder**: the body part that holds urine.
- **Urethra**: the tube through which urine leaves the body.
- **Nephron**: a tiny unit inside the kidney that filters blood.
- **Filter**: to separate the unwanted parts from something.
- **Urine**: liquid waste that the kidneys make.
- **Sweat**: liquid that comes out of the skin and carries water out of the body.

## Practice questions

1. Name the four parts of the excretory system.
2. What do nephrons do in the kidney?
3. Why does the body need water every day?
4. Name two ways in which water leaves the body.
5. A child plays in the sun and sweats a lot. Why should the child drink enough water?

## Answers

1. The four parts are the kidneys, ureters, urinary bladder and urethra.
2. Nephrons filter the blood and form urine.
3. About 70 percent of the body is water, and the body uses water every day, so we must drink it regularly.
4. Water leaves the body as sweat and as urine.
5. Sweat takes water out of the body, so the child must drink water to replace it.
