## What you already know
You may have pressed number and letter keys on a mobile phone or a calculator. You have also seen a TV screen, which shows pictures and words. In this lesson, a computer's keys and screen work in a similar way.

## Lesson
A computer has parts that work together. In this lesson we learn about three of them: the input unit, the Central Processing Unit (CPU), and the output unit.

**The input unit** sends data and commands to the computer for processing. Data is information, such as numbers and letters. A command tells the computer what to do. Processing means the computer works on the data. The devices we use to put data into a computer are called **input devices**. Keyboard, mouse, scanner, barcode reader, microphone, web camera and light pen are some input devices.

**The keyboard** helps us enter data. It has two types of keys. Keys with numbers are called number keys. Keys with letters are called alphabet keys.

**The mouse** is an essential part of the computer. It has two buttons and a scroll ball in the middle. The mouse moves the **pointer** on the computer screen. The right button is used to select files and open folders. The left button is used to make corrections in a file. The scroll ball moves the page on the monitor up and down.

**The CPU** is the brain of the computer.

**The output unit** changes the commands the computer has received into characters that we can easily understand. Monitor, printer and speaker are some output devices. The monitor is the most important output device, because it is linked to the computer. A monitor looks like a TV screen. Letters, numbers, pictures, cartoons and videos are shown on it.

There are two types of monitor. One is the Cathode Ray Tube monitor (CRT). The other is the Thin Film Transistor monitor (TFT). Today, computers usually have TFT monitors. They take up less space and give out less heat than CRT monitors.

## Key words
- **input device**: a device we use to put data into a computer
- **data**: information, such as numbers and letters, that the computer works on
- **command**: an instruction that tells the computer what to do
- **processing**: the computer working on data
- **pointer**: the arrow on the screen that the mouse moves
- **CPU**: the Central Processing Unit, the brain of the computer
- **output device**: a device that shows us the computer's results, such as a monitor
- **monitor**: the screen that shows the output of a computer
- **CRT**: one type of monitor (its full name is Cathode Ray Tube)
- **TFT**: one type of monitor (its full name is Thin Film Transistor), used in computers today

## Practice questions
1. Name two input devices.
2. What are the two types of keys on a keyboard?
3. What does the right button of the mouse do?
4. Why do computers today usually have TFT monitors?
5. If you want to type your name into a computer, which device will you use to type it, and which device will show it to you on the screen?

## Answers
1. Keyboard and mouse are input devices. (Any two of the input devices in the lesson are correct.)
2. The two types of keys are number keys and alphabet keys.
3. The right button is used to select files and to open folders.
4. TFT monitors take up less space and give out less heat than CRT monitors.
5. You will type your name using the keyboard, and the monitor will show it on the screen.
