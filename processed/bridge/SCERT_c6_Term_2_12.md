## What you already know

You have learned that a thermometer measures temperature in degrees Celsius (°C). You also know that a kettle gets hot when you heat water on it. This lesson uses those ideas to solve two short number problems about heat.

## Lesson

Today we solve number problems about heat. Heat is a form of energy. Heat energy can make water hotter. We measure heat energy in a unit called the **calorie**.

**Step 1: Read the question slowly.** Ask yourself: What is the same? What is different?

**Problem 1: Gas or electricity?**

Two friends each heat water in a kettle.

- Your kettle has **1 litre** of cold water. A litre is a unit for measuring how much liquid there is. It is heated on a gas stove.
- Your friend's kettle has **½ litre** (half a litre) of cold water. It is an electric kettle.

Both kettles take **5 minutes** to reach the **boiling point**. The boiling point is the temperature at which water starts to boil.

Look at what is the same: the time, 5 minutes. Look at what is different: the amount of water, and the kind of supply (gas or electricity).

The question asks which supply gives more heat in 5 minutes. It also asks how many times as much. To answer, you need the amount of heat each kettle gets. This passage does not give those amounts, so we cannot give a number for "how many times" from this passage alone.

**Problem 2: Heat needed for a rise in temperature**

The question says that **one calorie** of heat energy raises the temperature of water up to **31 °C**. Then it asks how much heat is needed to raise the temperature from **30 °C to 35 °C**.

Find the rise in temperature by subtracting: 35 − 30 = 5. The temperature rises by **5 degrees**.

**Remember:** Always find what changed first. Then use the heat information in the question.

## Key words

- **heat energy**: energy that makes things hotter.
- **calorie**: a unit used to measure heat energy.
- **temperature**: how hot or cold something is, measured in °C.
- **boiling point**: the temperature at which water starts to boil.
- **litre**: a unit for measuring how much liquid there is.

## Practice questions

1. What unit is used to measure heat energy in problem 2?
2. How long does each kettle take to reach boiling point in problem 1?
3. How much water is in the gas kettle and how much is in the electric kettle?
4. In problem 2, the temperature rises from 30 °C to 35 °C. By how many degrees does it rise?

## Answers

1. The unit is the calorie.
2. Each kettle takes 5 minutes to reach boiling point.
3. The gas kettle has 1 litre of water. The electric kettle has half a litre (½ litre) of water.
4. The temperature rises by 5 degrees, because 35 − 30 = 5.
