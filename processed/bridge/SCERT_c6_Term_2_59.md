## What you already know
You eat food every day, and after you swallow it, your body has to change it before it can use it. Think about how a big piece of rice or a whole fruit becomes small enough for your body to use. That change is what this lesson is about.

## Lesson
Your body has a **digestive system**. This system is made of two kinds of parts: a long food tube and some helper glands.

The long food tube is called the **alimentary canal**. Your body needs this tube because food must travel through it and change on the way. The alimentary canal is about 9 meters long. That is longer than a classroom!

The food tube has helpers. These are the **digestive glands**: the **salivary glands**, the **liver**, and the **pancreas**. Glands are body parts that make and release something. These glands release **enzymes**. Enzymes are special substances that help break food into smaller pieces.

The digestive system does two main jobs.

First, it changes **complex food** into **simple forms**. Complex food is food made of big, complicated pieces. Simple forms are smaller pieces that the body can use more easily. The enzymes help with this change.

Second, it takes in the digested food. This is called **absorption**. Absorption means the body takes the digested food into itself so it can use it.

The **stomach** is a major organ for digestion. An organ is a body part that does a special job. The stomach helps break down the food you eat.

Most absorption happens in the **small intestine**. The small intestine is a part of the food tube. The digested food passes into the body here, so this part is very important.

To remember: the food tube changes food, the glands help with enzymes, the stomach digests, and the small intestine absorbs.

## Key words
- **Alimentary canal**: the long food tube inside the body, where food is digested.
- **Digestive glands**: body parts that release enzymes to help digest food.
- **Salivary glands**: glands that help digest food in the food tube.
- **Liver**: a digestive gland that helps digest food.
- **Pancreas**: a digestive gland that helps digest food.
- **Enzymes**: special substances that help break food into smaller pieces.
- **Digestion**: the process of changing food into simpler forms the body can use.
- **Absorption**: the taking in of digested food into the body.
- **Stomach**: a major organ where food is digested.
- **Small intestine**: the part of the food tube where digested food is absorbed.

## Practice questions
1. Name the two main parts of the digestive system.
2. Name the three digestive glands that work with the alimentary canal.
3. What do the digestive glands release, and what do these substances do?
4. Why does the body need to change complex food into simple forms?
5. Your body takes in digested food from the small intestine. What does this mean for the food you eat?

## Answers
1. The two main parts are the alimentary canal and the associated glands.
2. The three digestive glands are the salivary glands, the liver, and the pancreas.
3. The digestive glands release enzymes, which help break food into smaller pieces for digestion.
4. The body needs simple forms of food because it takes in digested food, and simple forms can be absorbed.
5. It means the food you eat is changed into simple forms and then taken into your body so your body can use it.
