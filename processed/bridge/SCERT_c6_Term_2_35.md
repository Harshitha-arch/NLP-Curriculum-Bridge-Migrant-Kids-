## What you already know

From your NCERT book, you learned that air is a mixture of gases. Nitrogen and oxygen make up most of it, and we need oxygen to stay alive. Today you will learn how people first found out that air is a mixture.

## Lesson

For a long time, people thought air was one simple substance. This was true until the eighteenth century.

In 1774, a scientist named Joseph Priestley did an experiment that showed air is not a single simple substance. Air is a **mixture** of gases. A mixture is made of two or more things mixed together, but they are not joined into one new substance.

During this experiment, Priestley found a gas that had no colour. This gas was very **reactive**, which means it joins easily with other substances. The French chemist Antoine Lavoisier later gave this gas its name: **oxygen**.

Priestley's set-up was simple. He took a tub of water and placed a float on it. Then he put a lit candle on the float. The passage that we have stops at this point, so we will look at the next step when we have the full description.

Think about this. If air were only one gas, it would behave the same everywhere. But air is a mixture of several gases. Oxygen is one of the gases in that mixture. Priestley's work helped people understand this.

## Key words

- **mixture**: two or more things mixed together, but not joined into one new substance
- **gas**: a substance that spreads out to fill any space it is in
- **elementary substance**: a simple, single basic substance
- **composition**: the parts that something is made of
- **experiment**: a test done carefully to find out something new
- **reactive**: joins easily with other substances
- **oxygen**: a colourless gas in air that we need to live

## Practice questions

1. Who showed in 1774 that air is a mixture of gases?
2. What name did Antoine Lavoisier give to the colourless gas found by Priestley?
3. What did people think about air until the eighteenth century?
4. Why did Priestley's experiment show that air is not a single simple substance?
5. Your friend says the air in a classroom is just one gas. Using what Priestley showed, how would you correct her?

## Answers

1. Joseph Priestley showed this in 1774 with an experiment.
2. Lavoisier named the gas oxygen.
3. Until the eighteenth century, people thought air was a basic part of matter, a single simple substance.
4. His experiment showed that air is made of different gases mixed together, not one simple substance.
5. You would tell her that air is a mixture of several gases, not just one gas.
