## What you already know
You know that water can be ice, liquid water, or water vapour, which is invisible and is in the air around us. Today we learn what water is made of and why water from different places can be different.

## Lesson
Water is made of two smaller parts. Each water **molecule** has two atoms of hydrogen and one atom of oxygen. An atom is a very tiny particle. Scientists write this as **H2O**. This short form is called the **molecular formula** of water.

Water is a clear substance. It has no taste and no smell. It is almost colourless. But water is not the same everywhere. It can be clear or cloudy. It can have a lot of oxygen or not very much. It can be fresh or salty.

The amount of salt in water is called **salinity**. The salt is mixed into the water, and we cannot see it. Based on salinity, water is divided into three groups:

- **Freshwater** has 0.05% to 1% salt.
- **Brackish water** has up to 3% salt.
- **Seawater** has more than 3% salt.

Ocean water is made of many substances. Its salts include sodium chloride, magnesium chloride and calcium chloride.

**Try this activity to find salt in water:**

1. Take some tap water in a china dish.
2. Heat it until all the water has dried up.
3. Stop heating and look at the dish.

You will see small solid particles on the surface of the dish. These are the salts that were **dissolved** in the water. So, water has dissolved salts in it.

**Important:** Do not use distilled water, or water from a purifier or an R.O. (Reverse Osmosis) unit, for this activity. Use ordinary tap water.

## Key words
- **Atom**: a very tiny particle.
- **Molecular formula**: a short way to write what a substance is made of, such as H2O for water.
- **Salinity**: the amount of salt in water.
- **Dissolved**: mixed into a liquid so well that you cannot see it.
- **Salts**: solid substances that can dissolve in water.
- **Freshwater**: water with very little salt.
- **Brackish water**: water with more salt than freshwater, but less than seawater.
- **Seawater**: water from the sea, with more than 3% salt.
- **Deposit**: solid particles left behind on a surface.

## Practice questions
1. What is the molecular formula of water?
2. What is salinity?
3. Name the three groups of water based on salinity.
4. Which has more salt: brackish water or seawater?
5. A friend says there is no salt in a glass of water because the water looks clear. How would you show your friend that salt is present, using the china dish activity?

## Answers
1. The molecular formula of water is H2O.
2. Salinity is the amount of salt in water.
3. The three groups are freshwater, brackish water and seawater.
4. Seawater has more salt, because it has more than 3% salt, while brackish water has up to 3%.
5. Heat some tap water in a china dish until it dries up. Solid salt particles will be left on the dish, which shows the salt was dissolved in the water.
