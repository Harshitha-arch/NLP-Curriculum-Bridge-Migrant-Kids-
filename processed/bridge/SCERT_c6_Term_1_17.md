## What you already know

From NCERT, you learned that water vapour spreads out to fill all the space around it, even though you cannot see it. Today you will learn why this happens and why the smell of incense can reach you across a room.

## Lesson

**Let us do a test.** Put a book on a table and leave it alone. Then put a drop of ink into a glass of water. Do not shake or stir. Light an incense stick and place it in one corner of the room.

**What happened?** The book did not move. The ink spread out in the water all by itself. You also smelled the incense from where you were standing, even though the stick was far away.

**Why did this happen?** Everything is made of tiny bits called **particles**. In liquids and gases, the particles can move easily and quickly. The ink particles moved and spread through the water. The incense smell particles moved through the air to reach your nose.

**What is diffusion?** Diffusion is the tendency of particles to spread out so that they fill the space available to them. The ink spreading in water and the smoke spreading in the air are both examples of diffusion.

**Why does the book stay still?** Solids are different. In a solid, the particles are tightly packed, so they do not spread like liquids or gases do. A stone is a solid. A book is a solid too. This is why the book stays on the table while the ink and smoke spread.

**How are particles arranged?**
- In a **solid**, particles are tightly packed with very little space between them.
- In a **liquid**, particles are arranged in an irregular way, and there is more space between them than in a solid.
- In a **gas**, particles are far apart and move freely.

**Gases can be squeezed.** Imagine three syringes. One is filled with chalk powder, one with water, and one with air. When you press the plunger, it is hard to push down in the chalk powder. It is also hard in the water. But it moves easily in the air. So gases are highly compressible. This means they can be pressed into a smaller space. Liquids are hardly compressible, and solids are not compressible.

**Air is matter.** You cannot see air, but it takes up space and has mass.

## Key words

- **Diffusion**: spreading out of particles so that they fill the space available to them.
- **Particle**: a very tiny bit of something.
- **Solid**: a substance with a fixed shape, such as a stone or a book.
- **Liquid**: a substance that flows and takes the shape of its container, such as water.
- **Gas**: a substance that spreads out to fill the space around it, such as air.
- **Compressible**: able to be pressed into a smaller space.
- **Incompressible**: not able to be pressed into a smaller space.
- **Mass**: the amount of matter in an object.

## Practice questions

1. What is diffusion?
2. Why did the ink spread in the water, but the book stayed on the table?
3. Which is easier to press: air in a syringe or water in a syringe?
4. Why could you smell the incense stick from far away?

## Answers

1. Diffusion is the tendency of particles to spread out so that they fill the space available to them.
2. Particles in liquids can move easily, so the ink spread. Solids are tightly packed and do not diffuse, so the book stayed still.
3. Air is easier to press than water, because gases are highly compressible and liquids are hardly compressible.
4. The incense smell particles moved through the air and spread out until they reached you.

## Note for the teacher

This lesson is based on the SCERT "Diffusion" passage, with the particle arrangement and compressibility parts from the same unit. The passage does not say how long the ink takes to mix or how fast the smell travels, so the lesson does not give these details; the NCERT passages were used only for the "What you already know" link.
