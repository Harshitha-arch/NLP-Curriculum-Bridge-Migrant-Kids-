## What you already know
You have learnt that ice melts into water when it is heated, and water freezes into ice when it is cooled. Those changes are called melting and freezing. This lesson looks at two kinds of change: physical changes and chemical changes.

## Lesson
You already know that ice melts into water when it is heated. Melting is a change in the state of water. Ice and water are both still water. The substance has not turned into something new.

Scientists call changes like this **physical changes**. In a physical change, the physical appearance or state of a substance changes, but its chemical composition does not. No new substance is formed. Physical changes are **temporary**, which means they can often be undone.

Here are more examples of physical changes. Salt or sugar dissolves in water. A rubber band is stretched.

Now try an activity with an apple. Cut it into two halves. Then cut one half into pieces. Only the shape and size of the apple have changed. The apple is still an apple, so this is a physical change.

Leave the other half on the table for some time. Brown patches appear on the cut surface. This happens because some substances in the apple react with the air around it. A new substance is formed. This is a **chemical change**.

Chemical changes are **permanent**, which means they cannot be easily undone. The chemical composition of the substance changes, and a new substance is formed. Other examples are burning of wood, popping of popcorn, blackening of silver ornaments, and rusting of iron.

Some solids change straight into gas without becoming liquid first. This change is called **sublimation**. Camphor is an example.

Next, look at a **solution**. Take half a cup of water and add one spoon of sugar. Stir well. The sugar disappears into the water. The sugar is the **solute**, the substance that is dissolved. The water is the **solvent**, the substance that dissolves the solute. Solute plus solvent makes a solution. Water is called the **universal solvent** because it dissolves a wide range of substances.

## Key words
- **Physical change**: a change in the look or state of a substance, where no new substance is formed.
- **Chemical change**: a change where a new substance is formed.
- **Temporary change**: a change that can often be undone.
- **Permanent change**: a change that cannot be easily undone.
- **Sublimation**: the change of a solid directly into a gas.
- **Solute**: the substance that is dissolved in a liquid.
- **Solvent**: the substance that dissolves another substance.
- **Solution**: the mixture made when a solute dissolves in a solvent.
- **Universal solvent**: water, because it dissolves a wide range of substances.

## Practice questions
1. What is a physical change?
2. Name two chemical changes given in the lesson.
3. Why is cutting an apple into pieces a physical change?
4. In sugar water, which is the solute and which is the solvent?
5. A rusty iron gate is brown and flaky. Is rusting a physical change or a chemical change? Give a reason.

## Answers
1. A physical change is a change in the appearance or state of a substance, where its chemical composition stays the same and no new substance is formed.
2. Any two of: burning of wood, popping of popcorn, blackening of silver ornaments, and rusting of iron.
3. Only the shape and size of the apple change, and the apple is still apple, so no new substance is formed.
4. Sugar is the solute, and water is the solvent.
5. Rusting is a chemical change, because a new substance is formed and the change cannot be easily undone.
